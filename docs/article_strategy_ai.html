The Architecture

Three components. Anvil is the first. Your team builds the other two around it.

The Pipeline — Anvil

Described above. The engine that does the structured work. Open source, runs locally.

The Vault — what your team builds

Every Anvil run gets reviewed by a senior strategist. Strong reasoning, weak reasoning, overrides, internal data injected — tagged and stored. The vault is your institutional memory. Structured. Queryable. Permanent.

What goes in — problem framings that held up, hypotheses that survived, hypotheses that got killed and why, decisions that influenced the board, where human judgment overrode the model and why.

When a new strategist joins, the vault is their training ground. They see how every past problem was decomposed, what survived, what got killed and why. They learn how your team thinks in weeks instead of months.

The RAG Layer — what your team builds

Before every new run, the system pulls relevant past decisions from the vault and feeds them to Claude as context. This is a one-time build on top of Anvil — a retrieval layer that connects your vault to the pipeline. Claude reasons with the benefit of everything your team has done before. The output improves with every run. The vault compounds.

The compounding loop — every tagged run makes the next one sharper.

Is My Data Safe

This is the first question every security team will ask, and it should be.

Deploy Claude via AWS Bedrock and your data stays within your AWS region. Anthropic's contractual terms on Bedrock state that your inputs and outputs are not used to train models. It's the same infrastructure that banks and healthcare systems use for AI workloads today.

The vault itself sits on your own infrastructure — your cloud, your access controls, your retention policies. You decide what goes in, what gets retrieved for each run, and who can query it.

That said — do your own diligence. Read Anthropic's data usage policies. Read AWS Bedrock's terms. Have your security team review the architecture before production deployment. The contractual protections are strong, but "strong" is for your legal and infosec teams to confirm, not for me to guarantee.
