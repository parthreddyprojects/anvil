## Q1.11 — Environmental Consents and Run-Rate Permit Breach Risk

**ANSWER: DATA GAP — refinery-specific consent conditions require EHS Head confirmation.**

**EXTRAPOLATED — Structural risk assessment (reasoning chain):**
- Indian environmental consents under Environment Protection Act 1986 and CPCB/SPCB frameworks typically contain **minimum throughput conditions** in complex refineries because emission factors, effluent treatment plant (ETP) loading, and cooling water discharge temperatures are calibrated to a throughput range
- If run-rates fall below ~65% nameplate, **ETP hydraulic loading drops**, potentially causing ETP destabilization (sludge blanket collapse in activated sludge systems) — this creates a perverse risk where *reducing* throughput generates an environmental compliance violation
- **Stack emission permits:** Most refinery consent conditions specify emission limits in kg/hr (not concentration only); at reduced throughput, some flaring scenarios may create transient exceedances
- CPCB has previously issued notices to Panipat refinery for consent condition deviations (2019 incident — *Down to Earth*, October 2019) — establishes precedent for regulatory scrutiny

**⚠️ ACTION REQUIRED — By April 5 (Q1.11 deadline as stated in briefing):** Each refinery's EHS Head to review consent conditions for throughput-conditioned clauses and report to IOCL Technical Director.

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** Environmental consent conditions could legally constrain run-rate reductions below ~65% at one or more sites, and proactive notice to SPCB is preferable to a forced violation notice — this must be resolved before any run-rate reduction decision is communicated to plant operations.
