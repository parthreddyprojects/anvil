## 3.1 ESMA-Mandated Minimum LPG Production Volumes per Refinery

**BOTTOM LINE: ESMA sets no refinery-specific LPG production quotas. The mandate operates through distribution, not production. MoPNG allocation letters—not ESMA—are the operative instrument.**

- **ESMA (Essential Services Maintenance Act, 1968):** Prevents worker strikes/stoppages in LPG supply chain. Does **not** prescribe minimum production volumes at the refinery level. Source: C10 research, verified March 2025. **Confidence: HIGH**
- **Actual production mandate mechanism:** MoPNG issues LPG allocation letters under Petroleum Act powers to each refinery. These letters are **not public documents.** Source: C10. **Confidence: HIGH (that no public number exists)**
- **Refinery LPG output shares (FY24):** Paradip ~4% of product slate; Panipat/Gujarat proportional shares estimated at similar levels. At 300 kbpd throughput, Paradip's LPG yield ≈ 12 kbpd crude equivalent. Source: S2A (Paradip product slate). **Confidence: MEDIUM**
- **Alternative enforcement instrument:** Essential Commodities Act (ECA) 1955, amended 2020—government can direct OMCs to maintain buffer stocks and prioritize PDS supply. Criminal penalty: up to 7 years imprisonment for hoarding. Source: C10. **Confidence: MEDIUM**

**ACTION REQUIRED BY APRIL 4:** MoPNG's Petroleum Distribution Division must retrieve active LPG allocation letters for all four refineries and confirm the minimum production floor embedded therein. Until confirmed, assume MoPNG allocation letters—not ESMA—set the binding constraint.

**DATA GAP:** Refinery-specific MoPNG allocation minimums are non-public. Best estimate: LPG obligations likely set at 85–90% of historical monthly dispatch averages to distributor networks, but this is unconfirmed.

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** Do not invoke ESMA as the operative legal floor—obtain and act on MoPNG allocation letters as the binding minimum; ESMA is a labor instrument, not a production mandate.

## 3.3 Minimum CDU Utilization Thresholds—Technical Safety Floors

**BOTTOM LINE: Each refinery has a hard technical minimum below which restart costs exceed sustained operating losses; these thresholds are approximately 50–60% CDU utilization based on industry norms, but must be confirmed in writing by each Technical Director by April 3.**

**DATA GAP on written confirmations**—no public technical operating minimum documents exist for Indian refineries. The following are based on industry-standard engineering parameters applied to confirmed refinery configurations:

- **Paradip (Delayed Coker, NCI 11.8):** Most technically constrained site.
  - Delayed Coker: Minimum 55–60% feed rate to maintain coke bed thermal integrity and prevent drum quench cycle disruption. Below this threshold, drum cycle times extend to economically non-viable durations, and cold restart of the coker requires 10–14 days and ~$15–25M in restart costs. Source: Industry standard coker operating doctrine; applied to Paradip configuration confirmed in S2A. **EXTRAPOLATED; MEDIUM confidence on threshold, HIGH on restart cost order-of-magnitude**
  - CDU minimum: ~60% (180 kbpd) to maintain furnace tube skin temperatures above creep threshold and prevent thermal cycling damage
  - **Recommended minimum: 60% CDU utilization (180 kbpd)**

- **Panipat (Hydrocracker + FCC, NCI 9.4):**
  - Hydrogen plant turndown: Most hydrogen plants can operate at 40–50% minimum stable load; below this, H₂ purity degrades, forcing hydrocracker shutdown. Hydrocracker restart: 5–7 days, ~$8–15M. Source: Industry standard; applied to Panipat configuration from S2A. **EXTRAPOLATED; MEDIUM confidence**
  - CDU minimum: ~55% (165 kbpd) to sustain hydrogen plant above stable turndown
  - **Recommended minimum: 55% CDU utilization (165 kbpd)**

- **Gujarat (Visbreaker + FCC, no coker, NCI 8.6):**
  - Lower complexity profile means more flexible turndown. No coker bed integrity constraint.
  - CDU minimum: ~50% (137 kbpd). Visbreaker can be hot-standby at lower cost than coker. Source: Applied to Gujarat configuration from S2A. **EXTRAPOLATED; MEDIUM confidence**
  - **Recommended minimum: 50% CDU utilization (137 kbpd)**

- **Bathinda/HMEL (Delayed Coker + Hydrocracker, NCI 12.0):**
  - Highest complexity inland refinery; coker + hydrocracker dual constraint
  - MBPL pipeline (sole crude supply route) has minimum flow requirements for pipeline integrity (typically 30–40% of rated capacity for crude pipelines). Below minimum pipeline flow, wax deposition and drag reducer agent requirements increase sharply.
  - CDU minimum: ~65% (117 kbpd) driven by coker bed integrity + MBPL minimum flow requirement. **EXTRAPOLATED; MEDIUM confidence**
  - **Recommended minimum: 65% CDU utilization (117 kbpd)**

**ACTION REQUIRED BY APRIL 3:** Each refinery's Technical Director must issue written confirmation of the minimum stable CDU rate, coker minimum feed rate (Paradip, Bathinda), and hydrogen plant minimum turndown (Panipat, Bathinda).

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** Technical minimums likely sit at 50–65% utilization, meaning the decision space for run-rate reduction is 25–35 percentage points below current ~93% operation—not unlimited.

## 3.4 Positive Contribution Margin Product Streams at $156 Basket

**BOTTOM LINE: Petcoke from Paradip's delayed coker and bitumen from Gujarat/Paradip are the most likely positive-margin streams at export parity; naphtha is margin-positive only if exported; fuel oil is structurally negative at current IMO 2020 spec requirements.**

At $156/bbl Indian basket, domestic MS and HSD are sold at regulated prices generating ₹12–22/litre negative margins (C10, MEDIUM confidence). Export parity pricing for non-regulated products changes the calculus:

- **Petcoke (Paradip Delayed Coker):**
  - Paradip delayed coker capacity: 2.2 MMTPA (S2A, HIGH confidence). FY24 petcoke yield: ~8% of product slate ≈ 24 kbpd equivalent
  - Petcoke export parity price (API 2 benchmark, ~$120–140/MT coal equivalent): Petcoke trades at ~$60–80/MT internationally as of late 2024. At $156/bbl crude, petcoke variable cost of production ~$45–55/MT
  - **Positive contribution margin: YES, estimated $10–25/MT on export parity basis.** Source: S&P Global Platts petcoke index methodology applied to current basket; **EXTRAPOLATED; LOW-MEDIUM confidence**
  - Legal status: Petcoke export is legal; however, high-sulfur petcoke (>3.5% S) faces domestic use restrictions under NGT orders. Export is the preferred route. Paradip's coker processes heavy sour crude; petcoke likely high-sulfur. **Confidence: MEDIUM**

- **Naphtha (Panipat/Gujarat):**
  - Panipat's paraxylene/PTA complex consumes significant naphtha internally. Surplus naphtha at export parity: positive margin if exported to SE Asian petrochemical buyers.
  - Export parity naphtha spread vs. crude: Typically $2–8/bbl positive. **EXTRAPOLATED; LOW confidence on current spread at $156 basket**
  - Constraint: Gujarat has no delayed coker; visbreaker produces heavy naphtha—limited export-grade volume.

- **Fuel Oil (Panipat):**
  - Post-IMO 2020, high-sulfur fuel oil (HSFO) faces significant market discount vs. VLSFO. Panipat's visbreaker produces high-sulfur residue.
  - At $156/bbl: Fuel oil likely margin-negative or breakeven. **Confidence: MEDIUM**

- **Bitumen (Paradip, Gujarat, Panipat):**
  - Paradip ~5% bitumen yield (~15 kbpd); Gujarat has dedicated bitumen unit. Bitumen is a domestic infrastructure product; no export parity mechanism typically applies.
  - Bitumen prices are deregulated in India—positive margin possible if domestic spot price holds above cost. See Q3.13 for bitumen obligation details.

**ESMA/License constraint:** Shifting production toward petcoke/naphtha at expense of MS/HSD would require reducing FCC feed, which directly reduces MS production—potentially breaching ESMA distribution obligations. Legal clearance required from MoPNG within 48 hours of any such shift.

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** Paradip's petcoke stream is the most actionable positive-margin lever; maximize delayed coker utilization even at reduced CDU rates, but obtain MoPNG clearance before reducing FCC/HSD output below MoPNG allocation floors.

## 3.5 Export Permission for Diesel, Petrol, ATF Above Domestic Obligation Volumes

**BOTTOM LINE: Legal export of regulated fuels above domestic obligations requires MoPNG directive; precedent exists but 48-hour issuance is aggressive—realistically 5–7 days minimum through existing bureaucratic channels.**

- **Current framework:** India does not maintain a statutory export ban on petroleum products. However, MoPNG's "domestic supply-first" directive operates through administrative allocation, not statute. OMCs require MoPNG concurrence to divert above-obligation volumes to export. Source: MoPNG operational framework, constitutional practice. **Confidence: HIGH**
- **Precedent:** India exported petroleum products during COVID demand collapse (2020) when domestic demand fell below refinery minimum run-rates. MoPNG issued facilitative orders within 7–10 business days. Source: PPAC Annual Report 2020–21. **Confidence: MEDIUM**
- **ATF:** Less constrained—ATF has no domestic price regulation post-deregulation; airlines purchase at market rates. Export of surplus ATF to international carriers at Indian airports effectively constitutes export parity realization already. **Confidence: HIGH**
- **Diesel/petrol export:** At $156/bbl basket, export parity diesel would fetch approximately ₹105–115/litre vs. regulated domestic price of ₹87.62/litre (C10, HIGH confidence)—creating a ₹18–27/litre export premium. This is the core financial case.
- **48-hour MoPNG issuance:** Possible only via Minister-level direct order bypassing standard inter-ministerial consultation. Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA) is not required for export facilitation orders (only for SPR release per C05). Realistically achievable in **5–7 days** through MoPNG Secretary-level directive. **Confidence: MEDIUM**

**ACTION REQUIRED IMMEDIATELY:** MoPNG Secretary to initiate export facilitation order drafting now; target signature within 72 hours, implementation within 7 days.

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** Export permission for surplus diesel/petrol above domestic obligations is the single highest-value financial relief available within the refinery's control—MoPNG must prioritize this order above all other administrative actions.

## 3.9 IAF/AAI ATF Supply Commitments and Selective Curtailment Feasibility

**BOTTOM LINE: ATF for IAF and AAI is a strategic/defense obligation that cannot be curtailed; however, ATF yield cannot be independently controlled without affecting the overall distillation cut—selective MS/HSD curtailment below ATF production is technically constrained by distillation physics.**

- **ATF yield at Paradip:** ~7% of product slate (S2A, MEDIUM confidence) ≈ 19 kbpd at full throughput
- **IAF supply:** Classified strategic obligation; no public minimum volume. IAF fuel supply agreements between IOCL and Ministry of Defence are not public documents. **DATA GAP on volume; HIGH confidence that obligation exists**
- **AAI civil aviation:** ATF is deregulated; airlines purchase at market rates from OMCs. No ESMA-type mandate on ATF volume per se, but supply disruption triggers immediate operational crisis for Indian carriers.
- **Technical feasibility of selective curtailment:** ATF is the middle distillate cut from CDU atmospheric column (typically 150–250°C boiling range). It cannot be "turned off" independently of CDU operation. At any CDU utilization rate, ATF yield is approximately proportional to throughput. **Confidence: HIGH (distillation physics)**
- **Strategic reserve obligation for ATF:** No confirmed public strategic reserve requirement specifically for ATF. MoD likely maintains its own forward stocks at IAF bases. **DATA GAP**
- **Implication:** Any CDU reduction proportionally reduces ATF output. IAF must be briefed on the proportional reduction and pre-approved by MoD before any throughput cut.

**ACTION REQUIRED BY APRIL 4:** IOCL to brief MoD/IAF on proportional ATF reduction implications of any CDU curtailment; obtain written acknowledgment of acceptable minimum ATF supply levels.

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** ATF cannot be selectively maintained while curtailing MS/HSD—the distillation constraint means IAF/AAI briefing and MoD clearance are prerequisites to any CDU reduction decision.

## 3.12 Hydrogen Plant Constraints and BS-VI Compliance at Reduced CDU Rates

**BOTTOM LINE: A 25–35% CDU reduction at Panipat or Paradip risks H₂ supply constraints that could force production of off-spec sub-BS-VI diesel—which is illegal to sell domestically; each site has a minimum CDU rate above which BS-VI compliance can be maintained continuously.**

- **BS-VI sulfur limit:** 10 ppm sulfur (equivalent to Euro VI). In force since April 1, 2020 nationally. Violation is a criminal offense under Environment Protection Act 1986. Source: MoPNG notification 2020; CPCB. **Confidence: HIGH**
- **Hydrogen demand for hydrodesulfurization (HDS):**
  - Panipat: Hydrocracker + diesel hydrotreater are the primary H₂ consumers. At full throughput, H₂ plant operates at design capacity. At 65% CDU, H₂ demand falls proportionally—but H₂ plant has a minimum stable operating point of ~40–50% of design capacity. **EXTRAPOLATED; MEDIUM confidence**
  - The risk: If CDU is cut to 65% but H₂ plant is brought to 50% minimum, H₂ availability per barrel of diesel may actually **improve** (H₂ per unit of diesel feed increases). However, if CDU cut is abrupt and H₂ plant follows with a lag, temporary H₂ shortage can occur.
  - **Critical constraint:** If FCC is running at reduced feed but visbreaker/HDS still processing same volume of residue, H₂ demand per CDU barrel **increases** at lower utilization due to worse feed quality per unit. This is the dangerous scenario.

- **Paradip:** Hydrocracker + diesel hydrotreater similarly H₂-dependent. Paradip's 11.8 NCI (S2A, HIGH confidence) implies heavy H₂ consumption. H₂ plant minimum turndown: ~50–55% of design rate. **EXTRAPOLATED; MEDIUM confidence**

- **Minimum CDU rate for BS-VI compliance (EXTRAPOLATED):**
  - Panipat: ~60% CDU (180 kbpd) — below this, H₂ balance for HDS becomes uncertain
  - Paradip: ~62% CDU (186 kbpd) — coker and hydrocracker H₂ demands converge below this point
  - Source: Applied to S2A refinery configurations using standard H₂ plant turndown doctrine. **EXTRAPOLATED; LOW-MEDIUM confidence**

**ACTION REQUIRED BY APRIL 5:** Panipat and Paradip process engineering teams to confirm: (a) H₂ plant minimum stable throughput, (b) minimum HDS feed rate for BS-VI compliance, (c) minimum CDU rate that sustains both simultaneously. Written confirmation required.

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** Do not reduce Panipat or Paradip below ~60–62% CDU utilization without an engineering-confirmed H₂ balance calculation—producing sub-BS-VI diesel creates criminal liability that dwarfs any margin loss.
