## 2.3 — Russian ESPO via Kozmino: Legal Risk After April 3 Waiver Expiry

**Bottom line: ESPO via Kozmino carries meaningfully lower secondary sanctions risk than Urals, but this distinction is legally fragile, not codified, and IOCL must obtain written legal counsel by April 3 before committing to post-waiver ESPO purchases.**

**The critical legal architecture:**

- **No formal OFAC waiver with an April 3 expiry date exists in the public record.** Indian purchases of Russian crude have operated under US policy forbearance/non-enforcement posture, not a published OFAC instrument (OFAC FAQ #1025; Wilson Center March 2024 | **Confidence: HIGH**)
- Indian entities are not US persons; therefore **OFAC primary sanctions do not apply.** The risk is exclusively **CAATSA Section 228 secondary sanctions** and **Executive Order 14024** exposure (OFAC SDN/CAATSA documentation; Reuters 2022–2024 | **Confidence: HIGH**)
- **G7 Price Cap framework**: ESPO trading above $60/bbl means Indian refiners cannot use Western shipping, insurance, or financing. At $156/bbl basket, ESPO (typically trading at $8–12/bbl discount to Brent) will be ~$144–148/bbl — far above the $60 cap. Indian refiners must use exclusively non-Western tankers and insurers (EU Council Regulation 2022/1904 | **Confidence: HIGH**)

**ESPO vs. Urals distinction — the legal argument:**
- ESPO loads at **Kozmino, Pacific coast** — a non-Hormuz route, and structurally separate from Baltic/Black Sea Urals trade. Key factors that differentiate ESPO exposure:
  1. Kozmino-loading ESPO has historically transacted without Western intermediaries (non-G7 tankers, Indian/Chinese insurers)
  2. ESPO does not transit Hormuz, reducing maritime chokepoint exposure
  3. No Nayara/Rosneft SDN nexus for IOCL (unlike Nayara Energy where Rosneft's 49.13% shareholding creates direct SDN entity exposure — S&P Global Platts; Reuters October 2024 | **Confidence: MEDIUM**)
- **However:** OFAC has not published a formal distinction between ESPO and Urals for secondary sanctions purposes. This distinction is currently a **legal interpretation, not a safe harbor**
- IOCL's Russian ESPO volumes: approximately **450–500 kbd** of India's ~1.75–1.8 mb/d total Russian crude intake (Kpler/Vortexa Q4 2024 | **Confidence: MEDIUM**)

**Action required:**
- IOCL General Counsel and MEA Sanctions Desk must deliver joint written opinion by April 3, specifically addressing:
  1. Does US enforcement posture shift meaningfully after [scenario] waiver expiry?
  2. Does Kozmino-loading confer meaningfully lower CAATSA 228 exposure than Urals?
  3. Which Indian banks (SBI, BoB, Canara) will process ESPO payments post-April 3?
- **Engage US-India Track 1.5 channel** (through MEA/State Department back-channel) to assess whether enforcement posture has formally changed

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** IOCL should provisionally continue ESPO purchases post-April 3 only if written legal counsel confirms the non-Western payment/shipping structure insulates the transaction — do not proceed without this written opinion in hand.
