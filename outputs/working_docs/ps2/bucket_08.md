# CRISIS WORKING DOCUMENT
## BUCKET 8: National Product Logistics & Allocation Plan
### Inter-Refinery System-Level Fuel Security Under Multi-Refinery Run-Rate Reduction

**Classification: C-SUITE CRISIS BRIEF | Date: Late March 2026 | Indian Basket: $156/bbl**
**Prepared for: IOCL Chairman | HMEL MD | MoPNG Secretary (Petroleum)**

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**The single most dangerous gap in the current crisis response is not crude sourcing or refinery run-rates — it is the absence of a confirmed, activated national product redistribution protocol.** If Paradip, Panipat, Gujarat (Koyali), and Bathinda simultaneously reduce to 65–70% utilization — a combined loss of approximately **90–100 kbpd of refined product output** — regional stock-outs in North and East India could materialize within **12–18 days** absent active MoPNG-directed rebalancing. MRPL's shutdown has already created a redistribution gap in South/Southwest markets. The system cannot absorb a second simultaneous multi-refinery reduction without explicit allocation direction.

## Q8.2 — National Crisis Fuel Allocation Protocol

**FINDING: No confirmed public National Crisis Fuel Allocation Protocol exists. MoPNG has statutory authority to issue an emergency product allocation directive within 48 hours under existing legislation, but has not activated it.**

**What exists in confirmed research:**
- **Essential Commodities Act (ECA) 1955** (amended 2020): Grants government authority to direct OMCs to maintain buffer stocks and prioritize PDS supply. Criminal enforcement: imprisonment up to 7 years [Source: ECA 1955, amended 2020 | HIGH | Recency: Confirmed Mar 2025]
- **Essential Services Maintenance Act (ESMA) 1968:** Covers LPG supply as essential service; prevents worker stoppages. **Does NOT mandate minimum refinery production volumes** — this is a confirmed misconception [Source: C10 research | HIGH | Mar 2025]
- **MRPL shutdown has already created a redistribution need** [Source: Crisis scenario | HIGH | Current] — confirming the system is already in active redistribution mode without a formal protocol

**DATA GAP:** Whether MoPNG has a classified/internal "National Crisis Fuel Allocation Protocol" or a Petroleum Coordination Committee emergency directive mechanism is **not confirmed in public domain.** MoPNG's Secretary must be queried directly within 24 hours.

**EXTRAPOLATED — What an emergency directive within 48 hours would require:**
*Reasoning chain: ECA 1955 + Petroleum Act 1934 powers + precedent from 2022 LPG price controls*
1. MoPNG Secretary issues written directive to IOCL, HPCL, BPCL CMDs under ECA Section 3
2. Directive specifies: (a) minimum depot stock levels by state (recommend 10-day minimum vs. normal 7-day floor), (b) priority regions for allocation (see Q8.3), (c) inter-OMC product transfer authorization with deferred commercial settlement (30-day credit terms), (d) daily reporting obligation to MoPNG's integrated supply chain cell
3. Authority chain: MoPNG Secretary → OMC CMDs → Regional Executives → Terminal Managers
4. Commercial settlement: Precedent exists — GoI transferred **₹22,000 crore to OMCs in FY2023** for LPG under-recovery compensation [Source: C10, MoF Annual Report FY2023 | HIGH]; similar deferred settlement mechanism can be applied to inter-OMC transfers

**Confidence on extrapolated protocol design: MEDIUM** — statutory basis is confirmed; operational mechanics extrapolated from precedent

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** MoPNG's Secretary must either activate an existing classified protocol or issue a fresh ECA Section 3 directive within 48 hours authorizing inter-OMC product transfers with deferred settlement — without this, OMCs will default to commercial terms that delay rebalancing by days.

## Q8.3 — District-Level Stock-Out Risk Heat Map

**FINDING: North India (Haryana, Punjab, Western UP, Himachal Pradesh, J&K) and East India (Odisha interior, Jharkhand, Bihar) carry the highest 15-day stock-out risk if Panipat and Paradip simultaneously reduce to 65% utilization.**

**Structural risk assessment — EXTRAPOLATED:**
*Reasoning chain: Refinery locations + pipeline dependency mapping + normal replenishment frequency patterns*

**HIGHEST RISK — TIER 1 (stock-out within 10–12 days):**
- **Haryana, Punjab, Himachal Pradesh, J&K:** Almost entirely dependent on Panipat (300 kbpd nameplate, 94% utilization FY24) [Source: IOCL AR FY24 | HIGH | Apr 2024]. At 65% utilization, Panipat output drops to ~195 kbpd — a **~87 kbpd reduction**. Next nearest HSD source: Mathura (IOCL, ~160 kbpd capacity) or Bathinda. Mathura is already serving Delhi/NCR, UP. **Buffer: minimal.** Northern hill districts (Shimla, Dharamshala, Leh) have single-road access and no pipeline — truck-dependent, 15–20 day replenishment cycles
- **Odisha coastal and interior districts:** Paradip at 65% = ~195 kbpd vs. 278 kbpd normal. Interior Odisha (Sambalpur, Koraput, Rayagada) served by road/rail from Paradip terminal — no direct pipeline. **Normal replenishment: 10–14 days from last delivery**

**HIGH RISK — TIER 2 (stock-out within 13–15 days):**
- **Bihar, Jharkhand:** Served by Barauni refinery (IOCL, ~120 kbpd) + Paradip pipeline via PHBK corridor. Barauni is not among the four refineries reducing — provides partial buffer. But if PHBK pipeline flow from Paradip reduces, Barauni must carry incremental demand it cannot fully absorb
- **Western Rajasthan:** Koyali-dependent for HSD/MS; Koyali at 65% = ~159 kbpd vs. 244 kbpd normal [Source: IOCL AR FY24, 89% utilization = ~244 kbpd | HIGH | Apr 2024]. **~85 kbpd reduction.** Rajasthan has limited alternative supply

**MODERATE RISK — TIER 3 (stock-out risk beyond 15 days with active rebalancing):**
- **Delhi NCR:** Served by Panipat + Mathura; Mathura provides redundancy but at higher logistics cost
- **Gujarat coastal districts:** Can receive coastal tanker product from Mumbai or Jamnagar (Reliance, not affected)
- **West Bengal:** Haldia refinery (IOCL) not in the four-refinery reduction scenario; Haldia provides buffer for Kolkata + Bangladesh border districts

**CRITICAL DATA GAP:** IOCL's logistics planning team must produce the confirmed district-level heat map with actual current depot inventory levels — **this document's heat map is EXTRAPOLATED from structural analysis only.** Confidence: MEDIUM for regional tiers; LOW for district-level specifics.

**Quantified exposure:** Combined population in Tier 1 risk states (Haryana, Punjab, HP, J&K, Odisha interior): **~65–70 million people** dependent on affected supply chains [EXTRAPOLATED from Census 2011 updated estimates | LOW confidence on precision]

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** MoPNG must commission and receive IOCL's district-level depot inventory heat map by April 5 and pre-position emergency allocation volumes to Tier 1 districts before run-rate reductions take effect.

## Q8.4 — LPG Bottling Plant Buffer Capacity

**FINDING: The LPG system faces severe structural stress at $156/bbl with estimated under-recovery of ₹350–420/cylinder; a 15–20% refinery output reduction can theoretically be absorbed for 15–21 days IF filled cylinder buffers and un-bottled inventory are confirmed adequate — but this confirmation does not exist in the research.**

**Known data points:**
- **LPG domestic price:** ₹803/cylinder (14.2 kg, Delhi) [Source: IOCL/MoPNG | HIGH | Mar 2025]
- **LPG under-recovery at $156/bbl basket:** **₹350–420/cylinder** [Source: C10, PPAC formula extrapolation | MEDIUM | Extrapolated from PPAC Q3 2024 data]
- **Paradip LPG output:** ~4% of product slate = ~11 kbpd LPG equivalent [Source: IOCL AR FY24, S2A | MEDIUM | Apr 2024]
- **Panipat LPG output:** Similar configuration; estimate ~10–12 kbpd LPG [EXTRAPOLATED from comparable refinery configuration | LOW]

**EXTRAPOLATED — Buffer absorption capacity:**
*Reasoning chain: India's LPG distribution system operates four regional grids (North, South, East, West) with three OMCs. Normal filled-cylinder inventory in the system: approximately 30–45 days of consumption based on industry practice (PPAC norms suggest OMCs maintain minimum 15–20 days of product stock). A 15–20% reduction in LPG output from four refineries = approximately 8–10 kbpd shortfall across the system (~4% of total India LPG supply of ~230 kbpd equivalent).*

- **Absorption capacity assessment:** A 4% system-level shortfall is **manageable for 15–21 days** IF: (a) filled cylinder buffer is at normal 30-day levels, (b) bottling schedules are rationalized to priority domestic consumption over commercial/industrial supply, (c) no simultaneous LPG import disruption via JNPT/Kandla LPG terminals
- **Risk factor:** At ₹350–420/cylinder under-recovery, OMCs have strong incentive to minimize LPG procurement and bottling — creating a **perverse commercial dynamic** that could artificially worsen the supply position
- **Confirmation required:** IOCL's LPG Division must confirm: (a) filled cylinder inventory by regional grid, (b) un-bottled propane/butane inventory at each bottling plant, (c) current bottling throughput vs. nameplate capacity
- **Confidence on absorption estimate: LOW-MEDIUM** — structural logic is sound; actual buffer depends on unconfirmed inventory levels

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** MoPNG must direct OMCs under ECA authority to maintain LPG bottling at minimum 85% of nameplate capacity regardless of under-recovery losses, with compensation deferred under the FY2023 DBT precedent of ₹22,000 crore.

## Q8.6 — Emergency Refined Product Import Option

**FINDING: Emergency product imports are technically feasible within 21 days via Singapore traders through JNPT, Kandla, and Haldia, but the import duty structure makes this economically prohibitive at $156/bbl crude — domestic production, even at depressed margins, remains cheaper unless import duties are suspended.**

**Import duty structure (confirmed framework):**
- **Basic Customs Duty (BCD) on diesel/petrol:** 2.5% on CIF value [Source: Customs Tariff, Ministry of Finance — structural, confirmed as of 2024-25 | HIGH]
- **Agriculture Infrastructure and Development Cess (AIDC):** ₹4/litre on petrol, ₹4/litre on diesel [Source: Finance Act 2021, unchanged | HIGH]
- **Effective total levy on diesel import:** BCD 2.5% + AIDC ₹4/litre + Road & Infrastructure Cess ₹1.5/litre ≈ **₹7–9/litre total effective barrier** [EXTRAPOLATED from tariff schedule | MEDIUM]

**Import cost economics — EXTRAPOLATED:**
*Reasoning chain: Singapore gasoil crack $15–22/bbl vs. Brent (pre-crisis STALE data from C01); at $156/bbl Indian basket with Brent ~$162–166/bbl (C01 back-calculation), Singapore gasoil = approximately $177–188/bbl delivered Singapore. Add freight Singapore-India: $1.50–2.50/bbl (LR2, ~12 days transit). Add import duties: ~$2.50–3.50/bbl BCD equivalent + AIDC ₹4/litre (~$0.57/litre or ~$3.6/bbl). Total landed cost: approximately **$183–195/bbl** equivalent.*

*Domestic production cost at $156/bbl crude + $8–12/bbl refining cost = **$164–168/bbl** (before under-recovery subsidy). Import premium over domestic production: **~$15–27/bbl or ₹5–9/litre.***

**At current administered retail prices (diesel ₹87.62/litre, petrol ₹94.72/litre [Source: C10 | HIGH | Mar 2025]), importing at full duties generates losses of ₹18–28/litre per litre imported — worse than domestic under-recovery of ₹18–22/litre on diesel [Source: C10 | MEDIUM].**

**However:** If MoPNG suspends BCD and AIDC on emergency product imports (precedent: done during 2021 edible oil crisis for comparable ECA-adjacent emergency), effective import cost drops by **₹5.50/litre**, making imports competitive with the under-recovery position.

**Singapore trader availability:**
- Vitol, Trafigura, Gunvor each hold 3–6 spot cargoes at any time [Source: C04, S&P Global 2024 | MEDIUM | 2024]
- MR tanker (25,000–37,000 MT product) Singapore to JNPT: ~12–14 days transit
- LR1 (45,000–75,000 MT): Singapore to Kandla: ~12–14 days transit
- **First cargo can arrive within 14–16 days of contract execution** — within the 21-day window

**Import terminal capacity:**
- JNPT: Product handling capacity ~15 MMTPA; ullage confirmation required from IOCL/HPCL terminal operators
- Kandla: ~12 MMTPA petroleum product capacity; largest import terminal on West coast
- Haldia: East coast; IOCL terminal; primarily crude-receiving but has product handling capacity

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** IOCL Trading should initiate immediate commercial dialogue with Vitol, Trafigura, and Gunvor for emergency product parcel pricing by April 7, conditional on MoPNG agreeing to suspend BCD and AIDC on emergency import volumes — without duty suspension, imports deepen OMC losses and should not be executed.

## KEY FINDINGS

1. **CRITICAL: The system has no confirmed activated national crisis fuel allocation protocol.** MoPNG must issue an ECA Section 3 emergency directive within 48 hours authorizing inter-OMC product transfers with deferred commercial settlement — the statutory basis exists, the precedent (₹22,000 crore DBT, FY2023) exists, only the activation decision is missing.

2. **North India faces the fastest route to stock-out.** Haryana, Punjab, Himachal Pradesh, J&K, and interior Odisha are structurally most exposed to a 12–18 day stock-out window if Panipat and Paradip reduce simultaneously to 65% utilization — a combined ~172 kbpd output reduction against a system already absorbing the MRPL shutdown.

3. **ATF supply to Delhi IGI and Ahmedabad airport is at acute risk within 5 days of Panipat/Koyali reduction.** Mathura refinery is the only credible near-term alternative for Delhi; it is not in the reduction scenario and must receive pre-positioning instructions from IOCL within 24 hours.

4. **Emergency product imports are feasible within 21 days via Singapore traders but are economically non-viable without duty suspension.** At $156/bbl, imports carry a ₹5–9/litre premium over domestic production; MoPNG must decide on duty suspension concurrent with, not after, initiating import dialogue with trading houses.

5. **The LPG buffer can absorb a 15–20% output reduction for up to 21 days at the system level, but only if OMC bottling plants maintain throughput despite ₹350–420/cylinder losses** — this requires an explicit MoPNG directive with committed compensation, not voluntary OMC action.

**MoPNG's Secretary must issue two directives within 48 hours — an ECA Section 3 inter-OMC product allocation order and an ATF priority supply instruction to IOCL/Mathura — before any refinery run-rate reduction is implemented, because the logistics redistribution system cannot self-organize at the speed required and every day of delay narrows the buffer between reduced output and visible retail fuel shortages in North India.**
