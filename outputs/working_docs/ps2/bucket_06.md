## 6.6 — ESMA Force Majeure Declaration: LPG Supply Protection

**BOTTOM LINE: ESMA does not operate as a force majeure instrument — the correct legal mechanism is Essential Commodities Act 1955, and MoPNG must issue a gazette notification, not an ESMA declaration.**

- **ESMA current status:** NOT INVOKED as of early 2025. ESMA prevents worker strikes in essential services; it **does not** mandate refinery production volumes or provide refiner legal protection against supply shortfall enforcement.
  - Source: Essential Services Maintenance Act 1968 (Central); verified March 2025 | **Confidence: HIGH** | Recency: March 2025
- **Critical distinction:** ESMA protects against *labor disruption*. Force majeure protection for *supply shortfall* derives from: (a) individual contract force majeure clauses, (b) ECA 1955 gazette notifications permitting supply rationing, (c) Petroleum Act 1934 emergency provisions.
  - Source: ECA 1955 as amended 2020; Petroleum Act 1934 | **Confidence: HIGH** | Recency: Structural
- **ECA enforcement exposure:** Under ECA, criminal enforcement includes imprisonment up to **7 years** for hoarding/black marketing. No defined statutory minimum production threshold for LPG — thresholds are set by MoPNG allocation letters, not statute.
  - Source: ECA 1955, Section 7; PPAC allocation framework | **Confidence: HIGH** | Recency: ECA 2020 amendment
- **Hormuz as qualifying force majeure event:** Iran-Israel Hormuz mining would qualify as *force majeure* under standard CISG and Indian Contract Act Section 56 (impossibility/frustration of contract). MoPNG issuing a formal gazette notification acknowledging Hormuz crisis as a supply disruption event provides OMCs documentary cover for downstream customer claims.
  - Source: Indian Contract Act 1872, Section 56; CISG Article 79 | **Confidence: HIGH** | Recency: Structural
- **Timeline for gazette notification:** Emergency gazette can be issued within **24–48 hours** via extraordinary gazette procedures. Requires Law Ministry counter-signature.
  - Source: Government of India (Gazette Publication) Rules | **Confidence: HIGH** | Recency: Structural

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** MoPNG must replace "ESMA declaration" with an ECA Section 3 gazette notification acknowledging Hormuz as a supply disruption event — this is both faster and legally correct, and must be filed at Law Ministry within Crisis Hour +24.

## 6.14 — DG Shipping / War-Risk Vessel Clearance: Paradip SPM, Vadinar, Mundra

**BOTTOM LINE: War-risk declaration by vessel masters is a commercial/insurance decision, not a flag state regulatory prohibition — but port state control complications are real and require a pre-emptive MoPNG-MoS(Ports) joint clearance within 48 hours.**

- **War-risk zone declaration mechanics:** Under IMO conventions (SOLAS Chapter V) and standard P&I Club rules, vessel masters declaring war-risk zones are exercising commercial judgment, not violating flag state obligations. Indian-flagged vessels (Shipping Corporation of India fleet) are subject to DG Shipping circulars but not recall obligations for war-risk navigation under current SOLAS framework.
  - Source: SOLAS Chapter V, Regulation 34; UK P&I Club War Risk Guidelines 2024; IMO MSC circulars | **Confidence: HIGH** | Recency: 2024
- **Port State Control (PSC) complications:** War-risk-declared vessels calling at Indian ports may face PSC inspection delays (Tokyo MOU framework — India is a member state) if: (a) vessel documentation is incomplete for war-risk routing, (b) crew welfare concerns under ILO MLC 2006, (c) cargo insurance documentation gaps due to Western insurer withdrawal.
  - Source: Tokyo MOU Annual Report 2023; ILO MLC 2006 | **Confidence: HIGH** | Recency: 2023
- **Indian-flagged VLCC capacity (SCI):** Shipping Corporation of India operates **~5–6 VLCCs** (Very Large Crude Carriers). India's total VLCC requirement for 4.7 mb/d imports: **~120–140 VLCC voyages/month**. SCI capacity covers **less than 5%** of India's VLCC requirement — the overwhelming majority are foreign-flagged, primarily Marshall Islands, Liberia, Panama.
  - Source: SCI Annual Report FY24; VLCC voyage economics (IEA) | **Confidence: HIGH** | Recency: FY2024
- **Paradip SPM specifications:** Handles VLCCs up to 320,000 DWT; single point mooring; no alternative deepwater berth for VLCCs at Paradip. SPM disruption = complete cessation of VLCC crude receipt at Paradip.
  - Source: Paradip Port Authority Annual Report FY24; S2A research | **Confidence: HIGH** | Recency: April 2024
- **Vadinar/Mundra:** Vadinar handles Nayara's crude (separate from IOCL scope but relevant for system-level crude logistics). Mundra is the origin point for MPPL to Panipat — **any Mundra port disruption directly strands Panipat refinery crude supply** within 4–6 days (MPPL linepack).
  - Source: HMEL AR FY24 (MBPL pipeline); S2A research | **Confidence: HIGH** | Recency: April 2024
- **Recommended joint clearance structure:**
  - MoPNG + Ministry of Ports, Shipping & Waterways (MoPS&W) issue a **joint operational directive** (not gazette notification — simpler instrument) within **Crisis Hour +36** designating Paradip SPM, Mundra, and Vadinar as **Strategic Energy Infrastructure Ports** during crisis period
  - Directive grants: (a) blanket PSC clearance expedite for war-risk-declared tankers carrying crude for PSU refineries; (b) DG Shipping instruction to port authorities to waive re-documentation delays for war-risk routing certificates; (c) Indian Coast Guard escort protocol activation for VLCCs on final approach to Paradip SPM
  - **Flag state recall concern:** For Marshall Islands / Liberian flagged vessels (majority of IOCL's chartered fleet), India has zero flag state authority. The ask to DG Shipping must be framed as port state facilitation, not flag state direction.
  - Source: SOLAS framework; Tokyo MOU; Indian Ports Act 1908 (port authority powers) | **Confidence: HIGH (legal framework); MEDIUM (operational feasibility)** | Recency: Structural

**DATA GAP:** No confirmed DG Shipping communication or MoPS&W directive found in public domain for this crisis period. No confirmed war-risk zone designation by IMO or classification societies for Persian Gulf as of research cutoff.

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** MoPNG must co-draft the port clearance directive with MoPS&W Secretary by **Crisis Hour +36** — waiting for a PSC detention incident at Paradip SPM to trigger action means losing 48–72 hours of crude receipt at IOCL's largest refinery.
