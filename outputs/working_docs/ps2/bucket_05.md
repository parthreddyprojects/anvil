## 5.14 — STATE GOVERNMENT INTERFERENCE RISK: CONSTITUTIONAL ANALYSIS

**[EXTRAPOLATED — Tier 3]**

**BOTTOM LINE: Central ESMA directives under Article 246 and Entry 33 of the Concurrent List give MoPNG's crisis directions constitutional precedence over state government directives on petroleum products. **Constitutional Framework (EXTRAPOLATED from established constitutional law):**

- **Article 246 + Seventh Schedule:** Petroleum products and petroleum regulation fall under **Entry 53 (Union List)** — "Regulation and development of oilfields and mineral oil resources" — giving Parliament, and therefore MoPNG's central directives, exclusive authority over crude supply, refinery operations, and product allocation | Source: Constitution of India, Seventh Schedule | **Confidence: HIGH**

- **Essential Commodities Act 1955 + ESMA 1981:** Central government orders under ECA Section 3 override state government orders on the same subject matter — this is settled law per **State of U.P. vs. Synthetics and Chemicals Ltd. (1991 SCR)**. A MoPNG ECA Section 3 order on petroleum product supply priority is **constitutionally superior to any state government directive on the same subject** | EXTRAPOLATED from constitutional precedents | **Confidence: HIGH for legal principle; MEDIUM for specific application**

- **Concurrent List Entry 33:** "Production, supply, and distribution of" coal, steel, and certain other commodities — petroleum products are partially covered. Where Concurrent List applies, **Article 254 gives Central law supremacy in case of repugnancy** | Source: Constitution of India Article 254 | **Confidence: HIGH**
