## 8.1 — THE COMPLETE DECISION DEPENDENCY MAP

**The dependency chain has five sequential gates. No gate can open until the prior gate is cleared. This is not a parallel-processing problem — it is a serial dependency chain with one junction point where parallel tracks become possible.**

### Gate Structure (Serial Dependencies):

**GATE 1 — Legal/Sanctions Perimeter Definition** *(Must close: T+6 hours, by 06:00 April 2)*
- **Decision:** Confirm precise legal exposure as of April 3 — is the risk a formal OFAC waiver expiry or a US policy forbearance withdrawal? The distinction determines enforcement probability and reversibility calculus.
- **Owner:** General Counsel + External Sanctions Counsel (Washington D.C. / London desk)
- **Why first:** Every downstream decision — procurement volumes, insurance, financing — is sized against enforcement probability. Misjudging the legal architecture inflates or deflates risk premium applied to all subsequent decisions.
- **DATA GAP:** No OFAC public record confirms a formal April 3, 2026 instrument (Confidence: LOW | Source: OFAC public database through Q1 2025). Best estimate: risk is secondary sanctions / enforcement posture shift, not primary OFAC action against Indian entities as non-US persons.
- **Interdependency:** Gates 2, 3, and 4 cannot be sized without Gate 1 output.

**GATE 2 — War-Risk Insurance and Tanker Cover Confirmation** *(Must close: T+12 hours, by 12:00 April 2)*
- **Decision:** Confirm which P&I clubs and war-risk underwriters will cover (a) non-Western tankers carrying Russian crude above the $60/bbl G7 cap and (b) alternative origin cargoes (West Africa, Brazil, US) on Western-insured vessels through current routing.
- **Owner:** CFO + Head of Shipping/Chartering
- **Why second:** At $156/bbl Indian basket, Urals trades well above the $60/bbl G7 price cap (Confidence: HIGH | Source: EU Council Regulation 2022/1904; G7 Price Cap Coalition FAQ). Russian crude procurement **must** use non-Western tankers/insurance exclusively. Without confirmed cover, no spot purchase commitment can be made — the crude physically cannot move on compliant vessels.
- **Interdependency:** Gate 3 (spot purchase execution) cannot proceed without Gate 2 confirmation. Gate 2 also constrains Gate 3's volume ceiling (number of available non-Western VLCCs).

**GATE 3 — Alternative Crude Availability and Compatibility Confirmation** *(Must close: T+18 hours, by 18:00 April 2)*
- **Decision:** Lock confirmed cargo availability from alternative origins — West Africa (Bonny Light, Girassol, Tupi/Lula), US (WTI Midland), Brazil — and confirm refinery compatibility for each grade against Jamnagar DTA/SEZ and Vadinar crude trains.
- **Owner:** Head of Refining + Head of Supply (joint decision, both companies)
- **Data anchors:**
  - West Africa spot window: estimated **2–4 cargoes (~1–2 mb)** loadable within 10 days (Confidence: MEDIUM | Source: Kpler, April 2025)
  - WTI Midland freight to India: **$5.20–$6.50/bbl**, transit 25–28 days (Confidence: HIGH | Source: Baltic Exchange/Platts, April 2025; EIA routing data)
  - Vitol, Trafigura, Gunvor each hold **3–6 prompt West Africa cargoes** at any time (Confidence: MEDIUM | Source: S&P Global, 2024)
  - Brazilian Tupi/Lula (28–30° API, 0.40–0.77% sulfur): compatible with Jamnagar and Vadinar medium-sour trains; Dated Brent **−$1.50 to −$0.50/bbl** (Confidence: HIGH | Source: Argus, Q1 2025)
- **Interdependency:** Gate 4 (throughput rate commitment) requires Gate 3 volume confirmation. Cannot commit to throughput increase against unconfirmed crude availability.

**GATE 4 — Throughput Rate and Run-Rate Commitment** *(Must close: T+30 hours, by 06:00 April 3)*
- **Decision:** Set refinery run-rate targets for both Jamnagar and Vadinar for the 30–45 day forward horizon, based on confirmed available crude volumes from Gates 2 and 3.
- **Owner:** Head of Refining (each company), ratified by CEO
- **Interdependency:** Cannot precede Gate 3. Feeds directly into Gate 5.

**GATE 5 — SEZ Domestic Diversion and Product Export Commitment** *(Must close: T+48 hours, by 12:00 April 3)*
- **Decision:** Determine product split between SEZ export and DTA domestic sale; execute product export forward commitments.
- **Owner:** Head of Supply + CFO
- **DATA GAP:** SEZ regulatory permission timeline is unconfirmed from available research. **Best estimate:** MoPNG/DGFT approval for domestic diversion from SEZ requires 5–15 working days under normal processing; in crisis conditions, emergency dispensation may be available within 24–48 hours if CEO-level engagement with MoPNG Secretary occurs at T+0. Do not model domestic diversion volumes without initiating this regulatory track simultaneously at T+0.
- **Interdependency:** SEZ domestic diversion modeling cannot be finalized before regulatory permission timeline is known. This is the one gate that requires a **parallel regulatory track** starting at T=0, not sequential initiation.

### Parallel Track (Initiated at T=0, runs concurrently):
- **MoPNG/Regulatory Engagement:** CEO-level contact with Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas to seek clarity on SEZ flexibility and any emergency policy accommodation. Cannot wait for Gates 1–4. Start immediately.

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** The CEO of each entity must approve and publish this five-gate sequencing calendar with named owners and hard timestamps within the first 6 hours — organizational ambiguity about sequence is itself a margin-destroying risk at $156/bbl.

## 8.2 — IRREVERSIBILITY MAP AND REVERSIBILITY PREMIUM

**Bottom line: Three decision categories carry irreversible commitment within 72 hours. Each requires a confidence threshold materially higher than reversible decisions. Failure to price the reversibility premium will result in stranded obligations if a ceasefire, waiver extension, or insurance withdrawal occurs mid-execution.**

### Irreversibility Classification:

**IRREVERSIBLE — HIGH CONFIDENCE THRESHOLD REQUIRED (>80% confidence before execution):**

1. **Spot crude purchases with fixed-price confirmation** — Once a spot cargo is confirmed at a fixed differential against Dated Brent at $156/bbl Indian basket, the buyer owns the cargo. Typical crude spot confirmation is legally binding upon fixture confirmation (no recourse cancellation without penalty of 2–5% cargo value). At $1.5–2.0M per VLCC cargo at current prices, cancellation penalty exposure per cargo is **$1.5–3.0M** [SCENARIO-PROJECTED based on standard trading house penalty structures; Confidence: MEDIUM].
   - **Reversibility premium required:** Spot purchases should only be committed at Gate 3 if alternative supply economics justify the premium even in a scenario where Russian sanctions enforcement is *not* tightened (i.e., the cargo must be economic on its own merits, not purely as sanctions hedge).

2. **Term contract signings (30–90 day supply agreements)** — Any term agreement with a new crude supplier (Sonangol, NNPC, Petrobras) executed within 72 hours creates a volume obligation extending well beyond the immediate crisis window. Ceasefire or waiver extension does not dissolve term obligations. **IRREVERSIBLE for contract duration.**
   - **Reversibility premium required:** Term contracts in this window should include force majeure and sanctions event termination clauses as non-negotiable conditions. Do not execute without these provisions.

3. **Non-Western tanker charter fixtures** — VLCC charter fixtures (spot voyage or time charter) are binding upon fixture recap. In current market with limited non-Western VLCC availability (DATA GAP: precise non-Western VLCC fleet available for India routing is not in provided research; estimate 80–120 compliant vessels globally per Clarksons-derived estimates), fixture cancellation carries 10–25% dead freight penalty. **IRREVERSIBLE once recap issued.**

**REVERSIBLE — STANDARD CONFIDENCE THRESHOLD (>60%):**

4. **Internal throughput rate adjustments** — Refinery run-rate changes are operationally reversible within 72 hours without third-party obligation, though they carry internal cost (energy, catalyst, startup/shutdown). Reversible if crude procurement falls through.

5. **Product export nominations (pre-fixture)** — Product export nominations to trading houses can be withdrawn prior to vessel fixture without penalty. **Reversible until fixture recap.**

6. **Regulatory engagement with MoPNG** — Initiating conversations with regulators creates no binding obligation. Fully reversible.

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** CFOs at both companies must explicitly apply a reversibility premium — requiring higher confidence thresholds for spot purchases, term contracts, and tanker fixtures — and must pre-authorize the specific penalty exposure ceiling (in USD) that traders may commit without board re-approval.

   - RIL's petrochemical integration at Jamnagar means its product slate is structurally different from Nayara's simpler refinery output. A product swap arrangement (RIL supplies naphtha/feedstock to Nayara in exchange for fuel oil/VGO) could optimize both refineries' margins without crude procurement coordination.
   - **Executability within 72 hours:** MEDIUM. Requires commercial term sheet and credit approval, but no regulatory permission. Trafigura's presence in Nayara's ownership structure creates a pre-existing commercial relationship channel that could accelerate term sheet execution.

3. **Shared tankage for alternative crude storage (Vadinar SPM/CBM):**
   - DATA GAP: No research confirms available storage capacity at Vadinar or Jamnagar for third-party use. Not actionable within 72 hours without facility confirmation.

**Authority to initiate:** The CEO of RIL (Mukesh Ambani or designated Group President, Hydrocarbons) would need to initiate contact with Nayara's CEO directly. Given Trafigura's role in Nayara's ownership, a Trafigura senior relationship manager could serve as a neutral intermediary to structure a product swap conversation within 24 hours. **The Rosneft SDN ownership of Nayara means RIL's legal counsel must clear any bilateral arrangement for secondary sanctions risk before execution** — RIL transacting commercially with a Rosneft-controlled entity carries non-trivial OFAC exposure optics even if technically permissible.

**Verdict:** A product swap or coordinated trading house approach is **theoretically value-accretive** for both parties, but the legal clearance requirement (RIL-Rosneft nexus via Nayara) and the 72-hour time constraint make full execution within the crisis window unlikely. A non-binding Letter of Intent for post-crisis exploration is the realistic 72-hour outcome.

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** RIL's CEO should authorize a single exploratory call with Nayara's CEO within T+24 hours, but must receive explicit legal sign-off on Nayara SDN-nexus risk before any commercial term sheet is exchanged.

|---|---|---|---|

**COMMUNICATION FAILURE MODES TO PREVENT:**

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** The CEO of each company must, within the first 2 hours of this crisis, personally name the Incident Commander, open a physical or virtual war room, and distribute pre-authorized financial authority limits to operational decision-makers — without this, organizational latency will consume the available margin window regardless of the quality of strategic analysis in Buckets 1–7.

## KEY FINDINGS

- **The five-gate dependency chain is serial at Gates 1–4; only the regulatory track (Gate 5/MoPNG) can run in parallel from T=0.** Attempting to execute crude procurement (Gate 3) before legal exposure is confirmed (Gate 1) and insurance cover is locked (Gate 2) is the highest-probability execution error and must be explicitly prohibited by CEO directive.

- **Three decision categories are irreversible within 72 hours — spot crude purchases, tanker charter fixtures, and term contract signings — and each requires a confidence threshold >80% before execution.** At $156/bbl, each VLCC cargo commitment represents $150–200M in gross exposure; a cancellation penalty on a single cargo could exceed $3M. The CFO must pre-define the maximum irreversible commitment ceiling before traders execute.

- **The minimum viable 24-hour action is legal clarity + insurance confirmation only; no procurement execution should occur before T+24.** The margin cost of this sequencing discipline is near-zero; the cost of reversing a wrongly-timed irreversible commitment in a scenario where the waiver is extended or enforcement is not triggered could be $10–50M per cargo.

- **RIL-Nayara joint procurement is theoretically value-accretive but legally complex given Nayara's Rosneft SDN nexus.** The maximum achievable outcome within 72 hours is a CEO-level exploratory conversation and a non-binding Letter of Intent; full commercial execution requires legal clearance that likely exceeds the crisis window.

- **Organizational communication latency — not market conditions — is the binding constraint most likely to destroy margin capture.** The 2–4 cargo West Africa availability window (Confidence: MEDIUM | Source: Kpler, April 2025) closes within 10 days; a 48-hour internal cascade delay consumes 20–30% of that window before a single trader call is made.
