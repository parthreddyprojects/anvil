## 9.1 — PSU OMC EMERGENCY SUPPLY → DURABLE TERM ARRANGEMENTS

**EXTRAPOLATED | Confidence: MEDIUM**

**Bottom line: MRPL's absence creates a 400 kbpd product supply gap in South/West India that IOC, BPCL, and HPCL cannot fill from existing assets without logistical stress. RIL and Nayara have a 90-day window to convert emergency supplier status into 3–5 year term arrangements — but only if commercial terms are locked before MRPL restarts.**

**Why the opportunity is real:**
- MRPL supplies approximately 9–11% of Karnataka and adjacent state product demand directly through HPCL's retail network (HPCL holds ~71.5% of MRPL equity). An extended shutdown forces HPCL to source externally — and RIL's Jamnagar complex, with 1.4 mbpd capacity and proximity to JNPT/Sikka port, is the natural swing supplier
- PSU OMCs have historically resisted term product purchase agreements with private refiners (competitive/political optics), but emergency supply necessity provides cover for procurement officers to justify commercial necessity
- The 2017–2019 period showed that IOC, BPCL, and HPCL locked in informal product sourcing arrangements with RIL during turnaround periods that persisted 12–18 months beyond the triggering event — **precedent exists**

**What to prioritize in the next 72 hours:**

- **Pricing formula:** Propose import parity pricing (IPP) minus 1.5–2.0% as the base formula — this gives PSU OMCs a justifiable discount to import cost while preserving RIL/Nayara margins above domestic refinery gate economics. Avoid fixed-price structures that create renegotiation friction
- **Contract duration:** Push for 18–24 month minimum terms with volume bands (floor/ceiling) rather than spot-by-spot confirmation. Volume bands protect PSU OMC supply security while giving RIL/Nayara dispatch flexibility
- **Relationship investment:** RIL's Head of Refining should initiate direct CEO-level contact with HPCL (given MRPL linkage) within 24 hours — not procurement-to-procurement. Frame as "supply security partnership," not vendor relationship
- **Product mix specificity:** ATF and diesel are the highest-value products for OMC term arrangements; prioritize these over naphtha or fuel oil in initial term discussions
- **Critical risk:** If MRPL restarts within 60 days, OMC procurement officers face internal pressure to revert. Embed a "supply continuity clause" — guaranteed minimum volumes even post-MRPL restart — as the key negotiating objective

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** The Head of Refining and Head of Supply at both RIL and Nayara must initiate CEO-level OMC outreach within 24 hours — not after April 3 — because the leverage window closes the moment MRPL announces a restart timeline.

## 9.2 — FORCED CRUDE DIVERSIFICATION → STRUCTURAL SUPPLY RELATIONSHIP BUILDING

**EXTRAPOLATED | Confidence: MEDIUM**

**Bottom line: West African, US, and Latin American crude producers are structurally motivated to lock in Indian refinery volume commitments at favorable terms right now — because the Gulf crisis simultaneously reduces their competition from Middle East grades and signals India's strategic need to diversify. This is the best term-contract negotiating environment since 2022's Russian crude pivot.**

**Why sellers are motivated today:**
- Nigerian, Angolan, and Ghanaian NOCs have seen Asian spot market share erode as Russian crude flooded into India post-2022 at steep discounts. **With Russian supply now at risk (sanctions waiver expiry), West African crudes re-enter the competitive frame**
- US crude exporters (particularly WTI Midland from the Permian) are operating at near-record export volumes (~4.5 mbpd in early 2025, EIA estimates) and are actively seeking anchor Asian customers to justify long-term offtake-linked pipeline/terminal investment
- Brazilian pre-salt crude (Buzios, Tupi grades) offers RIL's complex upgrading units a genuinely competitive heavy-sour alternative to Urals — Petrobras has actively courted Asian refiners since 2023

**Structural opportunity specifics:**

| Origin | Grade Fit | RIL | Nayara | Term Contract Lever |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US Gulf (WTI Midland) | Medium-light sweet | High | Medium | 12–24 month at Platts Dated ± fixed differential |
| Nigeria (Bonny Light, Qua Iboe) | Light sweet | High | Low | 6–18 month; NNPC motivated post-governance reform |
| Angola (Cabinda, Dalia) | Medium heavy | High | High | Sonangol seeking anchor buyers; term premia available |
| Brazil (Lula, Buzios) | Heavy pre-salt | High | Medium | Petrobras prefers term; willing to negotiate freight contribution |
| Canada (Cold Lake, WCS via US Gulf) | Heavy sour | Medium | Low | Logistics cost is constraint; only viable at >$6/bbl discount to Dubai |

**72-hour actions:**
- **RIL:** Initiate contact with Petrobras trading arm and NNPC Ltd. at SVP/trading director level — propose indicative term sheet for 2–4 cargo/month minimum commitment at competitive differentials. This signals seriousness without P&L commitment
- **Nayara:** Given Rosneft ownership and existing Russian crude dependency, Nayara's diversification is both strategic and reputational — Rosneft's trading arm has West African exposure; use this to access Angolan grades through existing counterparty relationships rather than cold outreach
- **Both:** Request MoPNG diplomatic facilitation for fast-track NOC-to-NOC dialogue — India's government relationships with Nigeria, Angola, and Brazil are strong and underutilized for commercial crude access

**Confidence caveat:** Freight economics for USGC and West African crude to Jamnagar/Vadinar add $2.50–4.00/bbl versus Middle East grades at current VLCC rates. Term contract value only exceeds spot opportunism if differentials are locked at >$3/bbl discount to equivalent Middle East grades — this must be the non-negotiable floor in any term negotiation.

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** The CFO must authorize treasury to pre-approve hedging frameworks for non-standard crude freight routes within 48 hours, or term contract negotiations will stall at the commercial desk level before they reach strategic significance.

## 9.3 — PERMANENT LOSS OF RUSSIAN CRUDE: LONG-RUN GRM IMPACT AND CAPEX RESPONSE

**EXTRAPOLATED | Confidence: MEDIUM-LOW**

**Bottom line: Permanent termination of the Russia sanctions waiver would impose a structural GRM reduction of $1.80–2.40/bbl on Nayara and $0.90–1.20/bbl on RIL at normalized crack spreads — a permanent earnings impairment that justifies a $400–800M capex program across both companies to restore crude flexibility and upgrading optionality.**

**Reasoning chain:**

*Russian crude discount advantage (basis for impairment estimate):*
- Post-February 2022, Urals/ESPO traded at $12–22/bbl discount to Brent at peak; by 2024, discounts had compressed to $6–10/bbl as Asian demand absorbed supply and Western secondary sanctions tightened
- Working assumption: sustainable Russian crude discount (absent sanctions) = $4–6/bbl to Brent equivalent; with full sanctions enforcement = 0 (market access eliminated, not repriced)
- **Net discount loss on permanent termination: $4–6/bbl on Russian crude volumes processed**

*Volume exposure by refinery:*
- Nayara: Russian crude estimated at 70–80% of throughput (Rosneft ownership structure, historical import data, HIGH confidence direction, LOW confidence on precise percentage) → blended crude cost impact = **$2.80–4.80/bbl on total throughput**
- RIL: Russian crude estimated at 15–25% of Jamnagar throughput (RIL diversified earlier than Nayara; MEDIUM confidence) → blended crude cost impact = **$0.60–1.50/bbl on total throughput**
- After accounting for partial offset from alternative heavy-sour crude procurement at competitive (but not Russian-equivalent) discounts: **net GRM impairment: Nayara $1.80–2.40/bbl; RIL $0.90–1.20/bbl**

*Annual earnings impairment (illustrative):*
- Nayara (400 kbpd × 365 days × $2.10/bbl midpoint) = **~$306M/year pre-tax earnings reduction**
- RIL (1,400 kbpd × 365 days × $1.05/bbl midpoint) = **~$537M/year pre-tax earnings reduction** (partially offset by petrochemical integration)

**Required capex response — conceptual approval within 30 days:**

| Investment Category | Rationale | Estimated Capex | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Crude compatibility upgrades (desalter, heat exchanger expansion) | Process high-TAN West African/Brazilian grades without yield penalty | $80–150M per refinery | HIGH |
| Vacuum distillation expansion (Nayara) | Increase heavy crude processing from non-Russian sources | $200–350M | HIGH |
| Hydrogen capacity addition | Enable higher hydrocracking to compensate for lighter alternative crudes | $150–250M | MEDIUM |
| Strategic crude storage expansion | Reduce spot market dependency; increase optionality | $100–200M | MEDIUM |

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** The CFO and Head of Refining must jointly commission a crude flexibility capex study within 30 days — the GRM impairment math makes a $400–600M investment program NPV-positive even at conservative crack spread assumptions, and **the longer the capex decision is deferred, the longer both refineries operate at structurally impaired economics.**

## 9.4 — BUILDING PHYSICAL CRUDE TRADING CAPABILITY: RIL'S STRATEGIC OPTION

**EXTRAPOLATED | Confidence: MEDIUM**

**Bottom line: RIL has the balance sheet, refinery scale, and geopolitical relationships to build a Vitol/Trafigura-equivalent physical crude trading book — the crisis creates the talent availability, counterparty relationships, and organizational mandate to accelerate this from a 5-year aspiration to a 2–3 year deliverable. Nayara, constrained by Rosneft ownership, cannot pursue this independently.**

**Why now is the inflection point:**
- Every crisis-period spot cargo RIL books in the next 72 hours is a trading relationship that can be institutionalized. The difference between a company that buys crude and a company that trades crude is organizational intent, not market access — RIL already has market access
- Aramco Trading (established 2012) and Petrobras' trading arm (Petrobras International Braspetro) both originated from refinery necessity and evolved into profit centers generating $200–400M/year in trading income at scale
- The 2025 crisis is generating exactly the market dislocation — wide bid-ask spreads, distressed cargo availability, arbitrage windows between Atlantic Basin and Asian benchmarks — that a trading desk converts into income rather than simply absorbs as procurement cost

**What RIL already has (competitive foundation):**
- Jamnagar's 1.4 mbpd capacity makes it the world's largest single-site refining complex — sufficient scale to act as a market maker in crude
- Existing JV/commercial relationships with BP (historical), Saudi Aramco (equity stake), and multiple NOC trading desks
- Mumbai and Singapore offices with existing commodity risk management presence

**72-hour identification priorities:**

1. **Talent:** Identify 2–3 senior crude traders currently at Trafigura, Gunvor, or Vitol's Singapore/Geneva offices who have Indian market expertise — the current crisis is demonstrating the value of this capability internally, creating the organizational mandate for a retention/hiring conversation
2. **Regulatory:** Confirm with SEBI and RBI whether a Singapore-incorporated trading subsidiary (preferred jurisdiction) requires FEMA notification or board pre-approval — this is a 48-hour legal desk task, not a strategic debate
3. **Internal mandate:** The crisis response team should formally recommend to the RIL board that physical trading capability be designated a "Strategic Business Initiative" — this unlocks capital allocation priority and HR flexibility outside normal annual planning cycles

**Constraint acknowledgment:** Rosneft's ownership of Nayara (49.13%) structurally prevents Nayara from building independent trading capability given US secondary sanctions exposure on any Rosneft-affiliated entity engaging in physical commodity trading with Western counterparties.

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** RIL's CEO should authorize a 30-day feasibility study on a Singapore trading subsidiary within the next 72 hours — not as a Q2 2025 action but as a signal to the organization that the crisis has elevated trading capability from "nice to have" to "strategic priority."

## 9.5 — ISPRL PHASE II AND STRATEGIC RESERVE POLICY: SHAPING THE REGULATORY OUTCOME

**EXTRAPOLATED | Confidence: MEDIUM**

**Bottom line: The Hormuz crisis will almost certainly trigger MoPNG to accelerate ISPRL Phase II (strategic reserve expansion) and potentially mandate minimum private refiner stockholding — RIL and Nayara must engage in the next 72 hours to shape this policy, or they will receive a regulatory burden designed by bureaucrats without operational input.**

**Policy trajectory assessment:**
- India currently holds ~9.5 days of strategic crude reserves across ISPRL facilities at Visakhapatnam, Mangalore, and Padur (total ~5.33 MMT capacity). IEA norm is 90 days; India's stated target has been 30 days for strategic + commercial combined
- Every significant Gulf disruption event (2019 Abqaiq attack, 2020 Hormuz tension, current crisis) generates parliamentary questions and PMO pressure on MoPNG to accelerate reserve build — the current crisis is the strongest such trigger since 2019
- **EXTRAPOLATED probability of MoPNG initiating ISPRL Phase II formal planning within 90 days of crisis: 65–75%** (reasoning: political salience + existing Phase II site studies at Chandikhol and Padur expansion already in MoPNG files)

**The private refiner risk:**
- MoPNG could mandate private refiners maintain minimum 15–21 days of crude stockholding on-site (analogous to EU emergency stockholding obligations) — this would impose $800M–1.2B in working capital burden on RIL and $200–350M on Nayara at current crude prices
- Alternatively, MoPNG could offer private refiners the **opportunity** to co-invest in ISPRL Phase II as commercial partners, with government-guaranteed storage fees and strategic fill priority — this is the outcome RIL and Nayara should be lobbying for

**72-hour engagement strategy:**

1. **Frame the ask correctly:** RIL and Nayara should approach MoPNG not as parties opposing reserve mandates but as "strategic infrastructure partners" — propose a public-private model where private refiners contribute storage capacity (leveraging existing cavern/tank infrastructure at Jamnagar and Vadinar) in exchange for government-funded crude fill and fee income
2. **Specific proposal to table:** Offer to reserve 3–5 MMT of existing tank capacity at Jamnagar/Vadinar for strategic crude storage under a government lease arrangement at ₹180–220/MT/month storage fee — this generates income for private refiners while giving government strategic reserve credit without full ISPRL capex
3. **Key relationship:** The engagement must be at Secretary-level (MoPNG) and ideally PMO Principal Secretary level given the strategic nature — this is a Chairman/CEO-level call, not a regulatory affairs desk interaction
4. **Coalition building:** Coordinate with FIPI (Federation of Indian Petroleum Industry) to present a unified private refiner position within 48 hours — a fragmented industry response will result in the worst regulatory outcome

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** RIL's Chairman and Nayara's CEO must personally call the MoPNG Secretary within 48 hours to volunteer a public-private strategic reserve partnership framework — the alternative is receiving a mandatory stockholding regulation designed without industry input that imposes $1B+ in unfunded working capital obligations.
