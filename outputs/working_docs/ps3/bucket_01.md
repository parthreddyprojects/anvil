## Q1.11 — Secondary Processing Unit Status & Crude Grade Constraints

**BOTTOM LINE: DATA GAP on current secondary unit operational status. However, structurally, partial secondary unit availability at low-NCI refineries narrows the already-limited crude compatibility window — particularly sulphur tolerance depends on hydrotreater availability.**

**Structural logic [EXTRAPOLATED]:**
- Low-complexity refineries (NCI 6.3–9.0) have limited secondary processing: basic vacuum distillation units (VDU), some hydrotreaters, no sophisticated cokers or residue upgraders
- If hydrotreaters are idled or running at reduced rates, maximum processable crude sulphur drops from ~2.5% S to ~1.5–2.0% S (to avoid product specification breaches on HSD Euro IV/VI)
- Reduced VDU availability limits ability to process heavier crudes (API < 28°) by creating residual fuel oil inventory buildups with no outlet
-- If visbreakers (where present — BPCL Kochi, CPCL Manali) are offline, high-viscosity residue from heavier alternative crudes becomes unmarketable, creating a back-pressure that forces CDU rate reduction
  - Source: EXTRAPOLATED from refinery engineering principles + NCI definitions | **Confidence: MEDIUM**

**Secondary unit presence by refinery [EXTRAPOLATED from NCI and public technical disclosures]:**

| Refinery | VDU | Hydrotreater | Visbreaker | Coker | Likely Bottleneck if Partially Idled |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HPCL Mumbai | Yes | Yes (limited) | No | No | Sulphur tolerance drops to ~1.5% S |
| BPCL Mumbai | Yes | Yes | No | No | Sulphur tolerance drops to ~1.5% S |
| BPCL Kochi | Yes | Yes | Yes | No | Widest envelope among 6; residue outlet via visbreaker |
| CPCL Manali | Yes | Yes | Yes (partial) | No | Visbreaker capacity limits heavy crude processing |
| IOCL Bina | Yes | Yes | No | No | Designed for medium-sour; sulphur sensitivity moderate |

## Q1.15 — Fuel Gas & Hydrogen Balance at Reduced Throughput (40–60% Nameplate)

**BOTTOM LINE: At 40–50% nameplate throughput, low-complexity refineries face a fuel gas shortfall because fired heater demand does not scale linearly with crude throughput — external LPG or natural gas supplementation will be required, adding $200,000–500,000/day per refinery in fuel costs at minimum run-rate.**

**Fuel gas balance fundamentals [EXTRAPOLATED from refinery engineering principles]:**
- Fuel gas at refineries is generated from: CDU/VDU overhead gases, stabilizer off-gases, and hydroprocessing purge gases
- Fuel gas generation scales approximately linearly with throughput (~80–90% correlation)
- Fired heater fuel consumption has a significant fixed component (~30–40% of full-rate fuel demand remains constant regardless of throughput) — maintaining furnace skin temperatures, steam generation, trace heating
- **At 50% throughput:** Fuel gas generation ~50% of normal; fired heater fixed demand ~35–40% of normal total → **net shortfall of approximately 15–20% of normal fuel gas requirement**
  - Source: EXTRAPOLATED from API RP 556 (fired heater design standards); Turner & Townsend refinery energy benchmarks | **Confidence: MEDIUM**

**Hydrogen balance [EXTRAPOLATED]:**
- Hydrogen at low-complexity refineries is primarily generated by catalytic reformers (naphtha reforming)
- At 50% throughput, reformer feed is halved → hydrogen production halved
- Hydrotreater hydrogen demand does not scale exactly linearly (depends on crude sulphur content — if switching to lower-sulphur non-Hormuz crude, H₂ demand may actually decrease)
- **Net assessment:** Hydrogen balance is likely manageable at 50% throughput if switching to lower-sulphur ESPO/Bonny Light; becomes stressed if processing high-sulphur alternative crudes at reduced reformer output
  - Source: EXTRAPOLATED from refinery hydrogen network design principles | **Confidence: MEDIUM**
