## 7.1 — CRISIS CELL FORMATION WITH BINDING AUTHORITY

**BOTTOM LINE:** No confirmed formal crisis cell with binding decision authority has been convened. This is the single highest-priority structural gap to close within the next 12 hours.

**DATA GAP:** No public record exists of MoPNG convening a formal inter-ministerial crisis cell with binding (non-advisory) authority for this scenario. Publicly available information confirms only standing coordination mechanisms (PPAC weekly price monitoring, Petroleum Coordination Committee).

**What exists structurally:**
- PPAC functions as an analytical/advisory body under MoPNG — it has **no independent spending or allocation authority**
- The Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA) is the formal body with binding cross-ministry fiscal authority, but requires scheduling and quorum
- MoPNG Secretary has administrative authority to convene inter-ministerial meetings but cannot bind Ministry of Finance or RBI unilaterally
- Source: Government of India Allocation of Business Rules, 1961; PPAC mandate (MoPNG.gov.in)
- **Confidence: HIGH | Recency: Structural/permanent**

**Authority to convene within 12 hours:**
- **PMO** has the unambiguous authority to direct all ministries to depute decision-making representatives to an emergency cell — this is the fastest convening path
- **MoPNG Secretary** can convene a Secretary-level committee under Rule 12 of the Transaction of Business Rules within hours, but Finance Ministry and RBI representatives would attend in advisory capacity absent explicit PMO direction
- A PMO-directed "Energy Security Emergency Committee" with CMDs of HPCL, BPCL, IOCL, CPCL, NRL, plus Secretaries of MoPNG/MoF/MEA, and RBI Deputy Governor (Financial Markets) could be operationally standing within 4–6 hours of PMO directive issuance
- **Confidence: MEDIUM (structural feasibility) | Recency: Extrapolated from precedent**

**Binding mandate requirements for such a cell:**
- Explicit delegation of CCEA authority up to a defined fiscal ceiling (recommend ₹25,000 Cr as threshold below which cell can authorize without full Cabinet)
- Written mandate covering: crude allocation priority, LC guarantee authority, throughput floor directives, price adjustment trigger
- Source: Constitutional framework, Article 77 (Government of India business rules)

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** PMO must issue a written directive within 12 hours naming the crisis cell, its members, its fiscal ceiling, and its binding mandate — without this, every subsequent decision in this bucket is advisory and subject to individual ministry veto.

## 7.5 — MINISTERIAL DIRECTIVE TO PSU REFINERY CMDs

**BOTTOM LINE:** CMDs of HPCL, BPCL, IOCL, CPCL, and NRL are currently operating without written ministerial direction authorizing continued operations at negative GRM — this is a governance gap that creates board-level liability and lender pressure.

**The negative GRM reality:**
- At $156/bbl, PSU refiner GRMs are effectively negative on diesel (~60–65% of output sold at controlled prices) and LPG
  - IOCL Q3 FY25 GRM: $5.87/bbl; BPCL: $5.23/bbl; HPCL: $4.11/bbl — all pre-crisis
  - Diesel under-recovery at $156/bbl: **~$15–19/bbl** vs. GRM of ~$4–6/bbl
  - Source: C09, company investor presentations | **Confidence: HIGH (base GRM); MEDIUM (under-recovery projection)**
- Without written government direction, company boards and statutory auditors will require CMDs to flag continued operations as a **going concern risk** — this triggers lender covenant reviews

**What a written ministerial directive must contain:**
1. **Minimum throughput floor** for each refinery (recommended: 85% of nameplate capacity)
2. **Crude allocation priority** — which grade, from which supplier, with what payment mechanism
3. **Explicit government fiscal support commitment** — quantum and disbursement timeline
4. **Immunity from board challenge** — language protecting CMDs from personal liability for decisions taken under government direction (precedent: Section 17 of the Petroleum Act, 1934 for government directions to undertakings)
- Source: Petroleum Act, 1934; Companies Act, 2013 (Section 166 director duty provisions)
- **Confidence: HIGH (legal framework) | Recency: Structural**

**Precedent exists:** During COVID-19 (2020), MoPNG issued written advisories to OMCs on retail price freezes; during FY2022 under-recovery period, MoPNG directed OMCs to absorb under-recoveries pending government compensation — but these were advisories, not formal directives with legal protection.
- Source: MoPNG press releases 2020–2022 | **Confidence: HIGH | Recency: 2022**

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** MoPNG must issue a written ministerial directive to each CMD by Hour 24, with explicit throughput floors, crude priority, and fiscal commitment language sufficient to withstand board scrutiny and lender covenant review — verbal guidance is legally insufficient at this level of financial stress.
