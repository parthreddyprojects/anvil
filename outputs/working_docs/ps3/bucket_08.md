## FRAMING: WHY DEMAND MANAGEMENT IS THE FASTEST LEVER AVAILABLE

**The supply-side clock is brutal.** At 40% throughput across six low-complexity PSU refineries, combined refinery stocks of ~10–14 days (C08: MEDIUM confidence) plus SPR of ~10 days yields a **~23–25 day total crude buffer system-wide** — but this assumes normal demand. Every percentage point of demand suppression extends that runway proportionally. Demand levers can activate in hours; new crude cargoes take days to weeks. This is the only intervention class with a sub-48-hour implementation window.

**DATA LIMITATION WARNING:** The research base (C03, C08, C10) is heavily supply-side. Product inventory data at the depot/retail level (D1.4) is explicitly not in public domain. All demand reduction volume estimates below are EXTRAPOLATED unless otherwise flagged. Confidence levels reflect this constraint.

## D1.1 — Emergency Demand Rationing: Odd-Even, Priority Allocation, Aviation Suspension
**T2-MODERATE | Answer: A sequenced three-tier rationing framework can reduce HSD+MS demand by 15–25% within 48 hours, extending product runway by approximately 4–9 days at the system level — but implementation requires a formal ECA directive issued within 24 hours.**

### Available Data Foundation
- India's retail petrol/diesel prices frozen since May 22, 2022 — no price signal mechanism available without political decision (C10: HIGH confidence, March 2025)
- Under-recovery at $156/bbl: diesel ₹18–22/litre, petrol ₹12–16/litre (C10: MEDIUM confidence, extrapolated)
- **Price signals are therefore unavailable as a demand lever in the 72-hour window.** Administrative measures are the only viable tool.
- Essential Commodities Act (ECA) 1955 empowers government to direct OMC distribution prioritization; criminal enforcement (up to 7 years) available for non-compliance (C10: MEDIUM confidence)

*Reasoning chain: India's HSD demand ~90–95 kbpd for road transport + agriculture; MS demand ~70–75 kbpd retail; applied demand elasticity from 2008 fuel crisis analogues and 2020 COVID mobility reduction data (international)*

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Priority-only HSD allocation (agriculture, military, hospitals, public transport) — suspend industrial/retail free sales | 24–36 hours via ECA order to OMCs | **12–15% HSD reduction** (~11–14 kbpd equivalent) | **+3–5 days** at 40% throughput | LOW |
| Odd-even dispensing at fuel retail stations (alternating vehicle registration days) | 36–48 hours via state government notification | **8–12% MS reduction** (~6–9 kbpd equivalent) | **+2–3 days** MS-specific | LOW |

The refineries with shortest crude buffers — Mumbai cluster (BPCL/HPCL, ~10–12 days crude cover per C08: LOW confidence) — benefit most from demand suppression since they face the earliest throughput crisis.

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** An ECA priority allocation directive to OMCs, executable within 24 hours, is the single fastest runway-extension lever available and should be issued in parallel with, not after, supply-side procurement decisions.

## D1.2 — Industrial HSD Demand Management: Captive Power, Mining, Construction
**T2-MODERATE | Answer: Large industrial HSD consumers in refinery hinterlands could realistically reduce HSD demand by 8–15 kbpd equivalent within 7 days under emergency MoPNG directives, with mining and captive power generation representing the highest-yield targets.**

### EXTRAPOLATED Analysis
*Reasoning chain: India total HSD consumption ~85–90 MMT/year (~235 kbpd); industrial/non-transport share estimated at 25–30% (~60–70 kbpd) based on PPAC consumption category data (historical); captive power + mining typically ~12–18% of industrial HSD use*

**High-priority industrial targets by refinery hinterland:**

- **NRL Numaligarh hinterland (Assam/Northeast):** Coal and tea estate diesel generators; ONGC upstream operations. Estimated captive power HSD use: **2–3 kbpd equivalent.** Natural gas (from AGCL/IGL network) substitution partially feasible within 7 days. Confidence: LOW
- **IOCL Bina hinterland (MP):** Coal mining (SECL), cement plants, construction. Estimated industrial HSD: **3–5 kbpd equivalent.** Coal/grid substitution for captive power feasible with 5–7 day lead time. Confidence: LOW
- **CPCL Manali hinterland (Tamil Nadu):** Port operations, auto manufacturing (Chennai), construction. Estimated: **2–4 kbpd equivalent.** Natural gas grid (GAIL) penetration moderate. Confidence: LOW
- **BPCL Kochi/Mumbai + HPCL Mumbai hinterlands:** Coastal shipping (bunkering), textile mills, construction. Estimated: **3–5 kbpd combined.** Confidence: LOW

**Realistic aggregate reduction:** **8–15 kbpd HSD equivalent within 7 days** — representing approximately **9–17% of estimated industrial HSD demand.** Full substitution is constrained by: (a) gas infrastructure gaps in MP/Northeast, (b) coal supply lead times, (c) industrial contractual obligations.

**Mechanism:** MoPNG can issue directives under Petroleum Act 1934 and ECA 1955 to restrict bulk HSD supply to non-essential industrial consumers. No new legislation required.

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** MoPNG should issue industrial HSD restriction orders to bulk consumers >500 KL/month in identified hinterlands within 48 hours, prioritizing captive power generators with grid or gas alternatives.

## D1.3 — Aviation Demand Reduction: Domestic Route Frequency Cuts
**T3-EXTRAPOLATE | Answer: A 20% domestic aviation capacity cut would reduce ATF demand by approximately 5–7 kbpd equivalent, freeing modest but meaningful refinery yield rebalancing headroom — but ATF represents only ~7–8% of refinery output at low-complexity PSUs, limiting the yield rebalancing benefit.**

### EXTRAPOLATED
*Reasoning chain: India domestic aviation ATF consumption ~7–8 MMT/year (~19–21 kbpd) per DGCA/PPAC data (historical); 20% capacity cut = ~3.8–4.2 kbpd ATF reduction; low-complexity PSU refineries produce proportionally less ATF than complex refineries given Nelson complexity index constraints; yield rebalancing toward HSD/LPG at these refineries is limited by distillation cut-point flexibility*

- **ATF demand reduction from 20% domestic capacity cut:** ~**5–7 kbpd** (including frequency reduction effect on full-flight demand, not just seat count). Confidence: LOW
- **Yield rebalancing headroom at low-complexity PSUs:** EXTRAPOLATED as **limited.** Low-complexity refineries (HPCL Mumbai, BPCL Mumbai/Kochi, CPCL, IOCL Bina, NRL) have constrained secondary processing. Shifting crude cut from kerosene/ATF fraction toward middle distillates (HSD) requires hydrotreater/vacuum unit adjustment — feasible but not zero-cost or instantaneous.
- **Estimated additional HSD from ATF yield shift:** ~**1–2 kbpd per refinery** if ATF production reduced 15–20%. Aggregate for 6 refineries: **~6–12 kbpd HSD equivalent gain.** Confidence: LOW
- **Non-trunk route suspension:** Routes with <70% load factor and no essential connectivity function are the immediate targets. DGCA has precedent from COVID-19 (March 2020) for rapid route suspension via NOTAM.

**Mechanistic constraint:** MoCA/DGCA can issue capacity directives to airlines within 48 hours. Airlines can operationally reduce by suspending schedule filings. Airlines will seek ATF price compensation — this creates a fiscal negotiation that could delay implementation.

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** ATF demand reduction has symbolic and modest practical value but is not the primary demand lever; its main benefit is yield rebalancing headroom at constrained PSU refineries, not large absolute volume relief.

## D1.5 — MoPNG/PPAC Product Demand Triage Framework
**T2-MODERATE | Answer: No formal pre-built triage framework exists in the public domain, but the legal architecture to operationalize one within 24 hours is fully in place under ECA 1955 and existing OMC allocation mechanisms.**

### Legal and Institutional Infrastructure (Available Now)
- **Essential Commodities Act (ECA) 1955:** Empowers government to regulate production, supply, distribution of petroleum products. Criminal penalties enforceable. (C10: MEDIUM confidence)
- **ESMA:** Prevents worker disruption in LPG supply chain but **does not set production quotas** — this is confirmed and a common misconception (C10: HIGH confidence)
- **MoPNG allocation letters:** Existing mechanism for LPG/HSD/MS allocation to OMCs. Precedent operational. (C10: HIGH confidence)
- **DBT to OMCs:** ₹22,000 cr transfer precedent in FY2023 confirms MoF-MoPNG coordination mechanism for emergency compensation (C10: HIGH confidence)

### EXTRAPOLATED: Triage Framework Structure
*Reasoning chain: Applying ECA distribution control powers + existing OMC allocation architecture + international triage precedents (IEA emergency sharing system)*

**Proposed Priority Stack (operationalizable within 24 hours):**

| Priority Tier | Product | Beneficiary | Mechanism | Estimated % of Normal Supply to Maintain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 — Non-negotiable** | LPG (14.2 kg domestic) | PDS/PMUY households | Maintain full allocation; no reduction | 100% |
| **Tier 2 — Critical** | HSD | Agriculture (kharif season), hospitals, military, state transport | Priority bulk allocation via OMC | 85–90% |
| **Tier 3 — Essential** | MS | Ambulances, police, fire services | Dedicated depot allocation | 70–75% |

**DECISION IMPLICATION:** MoPNG can operationalize a formal demand triage directive within 24 hours using existing ECA powers — the binding constraint is political will, not legal or operational capacity, and leadership must decide immediately whether to activate before first-come-first-served depletion begins.

## KEY FINDINGS

- **The demand-side clock is faster than the supply-side clock.** An ECA priority allocation directive can be operational within 6–12 hours; new crude cargoes take minimum 7–14 days. Demand management is the only sub-24-hour intervention available and must be activated in parallel with, not sequentially after, sourcing decisions.

- **A combined demand rationing package (industrial HSD restriction + priority-only retail allocation + odd-even dispensing) can plausibly extend the product runway by 5–9 days system-wide** — equivalent to the time needed for one additional crude cargo to arrive and be processed. (EXTRAPOLATED: LOW confidence, but directionally robust.)

- **The Mumbai hinterland (HPCL Mumbai + BPCL Mumbai) carries the shortest estimated total system inventory buffer (~13–18 days combined) and the highest absolute demand concentration** — it should be the first trigger point for demand rationing activation and the first priority for internal PPAC depot data verification.

- **The legal architecture for a 24-hour demand triage directive is fully operational under ECA 1955 and existing OMC allocation mechanisms** — no new legislation is required. The binding constraint is political will given the Bihar 2025 election cycle, not operational capacity.

- **CRITICAL DATA GAP: Depot and retail-level product inventory positions by hinterland are not in the public domain.** The PPAC crisis cell must pull internal weekly OMC depot stock submissions within 6 hours — without this data, all rationing trigger decisions rest on estimates, not confirmed positions.
