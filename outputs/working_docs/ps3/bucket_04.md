## 4.2 — MRPL SHUTDOWN TRIGGER AND REPLICATION RISK

### MRPL Financial Trigger — DATA GAP / PARTIAL

**What the research confirms:**
- MRPL reported GRM of $5.40/bbl in Q2 FY2025 (pre-shutdown). Source: MRPL Q2 Results, Nov 2024. **Confidence: HIGH**
- MRPL restart economics require crude below ~$140/bbl or crack spread recovery — internal estimate, **Confidence: LOW**
- The specific triggering mechanism (LC refusal, payment default, or board decision) is **NOT FOUND** in available research

**Structural analysis of likely trigger — EXTRAPOLATED:**

The three possible triggers and their applicability to operating refineries:

1. **LC Refusal (bank declines to issue letter of credit for crude purchase):** This is the most operationally immediate trigger. At -$15 to -$22/bbl GRM, banks assess refinery cash flows as insufficient to service LC obligations. SBI, Bank of Baroda, and Union Bank are primary LC banks for PSU refiners. Red flag: if any bank has internally downgraded refinery exposure or invoked credit committee review, LC conditionality could appear within days.

2. **Crude Payment Default:** Occurs when prior LC matures and cash is insufficient to honor. Precedes only if LC was already issued — meaning 30–45 day lag from LC issuance to default.

3. **Board Decision:** Voluntary operational halt to prevent deeper loss accumulation — likely precedes formal default to protect directors.

**Most probable MRPL sequence:** Board decision following internal LC committee refusal — i.e., LC refusal triggered board action. This sequence is standard in Indian PSU refinery crises.

**Does the same condition apply to the 6 operating refineries?**

- **DATA GAP — CRITICAL:** Whether any bank has declined or conditioned an LC in the last 72 hours is **NOT FOUND** — this requires immediate CFO-level calls to all 6 refineries and their banking relationships. This is the single most important data point in Bucket 4.
- **Structural risk is HIGH:** At -$18/bbl average GRM, PSU refinery credit profiles have deteriorated to levels where bank credit committees would normally apply conditions. The only mitigant is government ownership (implicit sovereign backing) which PSU banks may still rely upon.

DECISION IMPLICATION: MoPNG crisis cell must conduct emergency CFO calls to all 6 refineries within 6 hours to determine if any LC is being conditioned or refused — if yes, 24-hour government LC guarantee is required immediately.

## 4.4 — RBI/EXIM BANK EMERGENCY CRUDE IMPORT CREDIT FACILITY

### Legal and Operational Pathway

**Existing frameworks applicable:**

- **Exim Bank of India** has statutory authority under the Export-Import Bank of India Act, 1981 to extend lines of credit for import financing of strategic commodities. No new legislation required.
- **SBI** as lead PSU bank can extend emergency working capital lines under RBI's "Priority Sector – Strategic Imports" framework (informal mechanism; no published rule, but used in past energy crises).
- **RBI Emergency Liquidity:** RBI can issue a special refinancing window for banks financing crude imports — analogous to the Special Liquidity Scheme used in 2020. Legal basis: RBI Act Section 17(3A).

**Volume of crude purchases financeable in 30 days — EXTRAPOLATED:**

- Total 6-refinery crude intake at 85% utilization: ~1,093 kbd = ~32.8 million barrels/month
- At $156/bbl: **~$5.1 billion/month total crude cost**
- Government-backed credit facility needed to cover incremental under-recovery gap (not full crude cost — refineries generate product sale revenue): ~$4.8–5.9B deficit (from Q4.3)
- Exim Bank balance sheet can theoretically support ₹15,000–20,000 crore (~$1.8–2.4B) in emergency lines within weeks, not days. SBI could extend complementary lines.
- **Confidence: LOW — EXTRAPOLATED from Exim Bank FY2024 annual report capital adequacy and loan book size**

**Can this be done before April 3?**

- **Partial answer: YES, for sovereign-guaranteed SBI lines.** SBI can extend emergency working capital to PSU refiners under government direction within 24–48 hours if MoF issues a directive — SBI is 57.6% government-owned and historically responsive to ministry instruction in crisis.
- Full Exim Bank credit line: Requires board approval — minimum 72–96 hours even in emergency session.
- **RBI forward guidance** (instruction to banks not to restrict LC issuance to PSU refiners): Can be issued within 24 hours via an RBI circular. This is the fastest available instrument.

**RECOMMENDED SEQUENCING:**
1. RBI circular to banks (within 24 hours): Do not restrict LC issuance to PSU refiners pending government guarantee
2. SBI emergency working capital lines (within 48 hours): Under MoF direction
3. CCEA sovereign guarantee resolution (within 48 hours): Backstops all bank exposure
4. Exim Bank credit line (within 5–7 days): Medium-term facility

DECISION IMPLICATION: RBI should issue an emergency banking circular within 24 hours instructing PSU banks not to restrict crude import LCs for government-owned refineries — this is the single fastest intervention available before April 3.

## 4.13 — LPG UNDER-RECOVERY: SEPARATE COMPENSATION MECHANISM

### Current LPG Subsidy Architecture

**DBTL (Direct Benefit Transfer for LPG) mechanism:**
- Under the PAHAL/DBTL scheme, consumers receive LPG at market price from OMCs and receive subsidy directly in bank accounts (for BPL/eligible households).
- **Critically: OMCs receive market-linked price from consumers, not the subsidized price.** The subsidy burden is borne by GOI budget transfer to consumer accounts — **not absorbed by refineries or OMCs in the DBTL framework.**
- Source: MoPNG PAHAL scheme documentation; PIB. **Confidence: HIGH | Recency: Confirmed 2024**

**However — the DBTL market price is NOT the full trade parity price:**
- "Market price" under DBTL is set administratively by MoPNG, not by true import parity.
- Current LPG cylinder price (Delhi): ₹903/cylinder (14.2 kg) — this is what OMCs receive from consumers (subsidy beneficiaries get ₹200–300 cash transfer separately).
- Trade parity at $156/bbl: ₹1,450–1,550/cylinder — meaning OMCs/refiners still receive only ₹903, **not trade parity.**
- **Under-recovery borne by OMCs/refiners on LPG: ₹547–647/cylinder at $156/bbl basket**
- The DBTL cash transfer to consumers does NOT compensate OMCs for this gap — it compensates consumers separately.
- Source: PPAC DBTL scheme structure; C10 research under-recovery estimates. **Confidence: MEDIUM**

**Is LPG under-recovery cash-flow certain for April? — DATA GAP / PARTIALLY ANSWERED:**
- The government has historically compensated OMC LPG under-recovery through direct budget transfers — ₹22,000 crore transferred in FY2023 for this purpose (PIB, FY2023). Source: C10 research. **Confidence: HIGH**
- **Whether a committed April 2026 LPG compensation transfer is in place: DATA GAP** — requires confirmation from MoPNG/MoF whether FY2026 budget provision covers under-recovery at $156/bbl (it almost certainly does not, as budget was prepared at lower crude price assumptions).

- **Confidence: MEDIUM** — EXTRAPOLATED from DBTL structure and PPAC data

**Critical finding: LPG under-recovery is NOT automatically compensated in a timely enough manner to prevent the cash crisis.** Historical DBT transfers to OMCs occur with 3–6 month budget cycle lag. The ₹22,000 crore FY2023 transfer took multiple months to crystallize.

DECISION IMPLICATION: MoF must explicitly confirm within 24 hours whether an emergency LPG under-recovery compensation transfer is authorized for April — if not, LPG under-recovery compounds the MS/HSD solvency crisis and increases the required guarantee quantum by 20–25%.
