# PS1

**PROBLEM STATEMENT:**
Within the next 72 hours (by April 3, before the Russia sanctions waiver expires), what should the CEOs, CFOs, Heads of Refining, and Heads of Supply at Reliance Industries (Jamnagar, 1.4 mbpd) and Nayara Energy (Vadinar, 400 kbpd) decide regarding crude procurement source-switching, run-rate optimization, product export pricing, petrochemical feedstock locking, and balance-sheet deployment — in order to capture a margin uplift of at least $4–6/bbl above normalized GRM within the April–June 2025 quarter — given Hormuz mining that has suspended normal Gulf liftings, Indian basket at $156/bbl creating elevated crack spreads and working capital stress, MRPL's shutdown removing a domestic competitor and creating a spot product arbitrage window, and the simultaneous expiry of the Russia waiver that risks losing the single largest source of discounted crude for both refiners?

**BREAK POINT:**
The capitalize-versus-protect inflection point sits at $142/bbl Indian basket: above $142, crack spreads on diesel, jet, and naphtha remain wide enough that aggressive run maximization, spot crude procurement at premium, and petrochemical feedstock locking generate positive incremental margin even after war-risk insurance surcharges and elevated freight; below $142, the working capital cost of holding high-price crude inventory and the risk of a sudden ceasefire-driven crude price collapse make a defensive posture — reduced throughput, shorter crude procurement tenors, increased product hedging — the correct recommendation. A secondary sensitivity is Russian crude availability post-April 3: if more than 60% of current Russian volume is lost, the crude cost disadvantage exceeds the crack-spread opportunity at any basket price, reversing the run-maximization recommendation regardless of crack spread level.

- **[D1.1]** What emergency demand rationing measures — including odd-even dispensing at fuel stations, priority-only allocation for essential services (military, hospitals, agriculture, public transport), and suspension of non-essential aviation routes — could MoPNG and state governments implement within 48 hours to reduce effective HSD and MS demand by 15–25%, and what is the estimated product inventory runway extension (in days) that each measure would provide at each refinery's hinterland?
- **[D1.2]** Are there large industrial HSD consumers (captive power generators, mining, construction) in the hinterlands of the at-risk refineries that could be directed to switch to alternative fuels (natural gas, coal) or reduce operations during the crisis period under emergency MoPNG directives — and what volume of HSD demand reduction (in kbpd equivalent) could realistically be achieved through industrial demand management within 7 days?
- **[D1.3]** Can the Ministry of Civil Aviation temporarily reduce domestic flight frequencies or suspend non-trunk routes to cut ATF demand, freeing refinery throughput capacity for LPG and HSD production — and what is the estimated ATF demand reduction from a 20% domestic capacity cut, and does it create sufficient product yield rebalancing headroom at the constrained refineries?
- **[D1.4]** What is the product inventory position in the OMC depot and retail network (not just at-refinery stocks as in 1.5) for each hinterland — specifically, how many days of demand do the combined pipeline, depot, and retail-level inventories cover even if refinery output drops to zero today, and which hinterlands have the longest and shortest total system inventory buffers?
- **[D1.5]** Has MoPNG or PPAC modeled a product demand triage framework that explicitly prioritizes (1) LPG for domestic cooking, (2) HSD for agriculture and essential transport, (3) MS for emergency services, and (4) ATF for designated essential air routes — and can this framework be operationalized as a formal supply allocation directive to OMCs and state oil companies within 24 hours, enabling controlled supply rationing rather than a first-come-first-served depletion?

*The decomposition addresses financial solvency, regulatory waivers, and sanctions law in passing across multiple sections, but there is no dedicated bucket for the contractual and legal risk dimension: force majeure declarations on crude purchase contracts, product supply agreement defaults, offtake contract violations, insurance claim activation, directors' liability for continued negative-GRM operations, and the legal instruments available to MoPNG to direct PSU refiners to operate against their boards' fiduciary interests.
