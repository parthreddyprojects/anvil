IOCL & HMEL (Mid-Tier PSU): Crisis Brief

Decision Deadline: April 3, 2026 | Indian Basket: $156/bbl
Problem Statement

Hypothesis Graveyard

KILLED: SPR drawdown via CCEA authorization within 24-48 hours is a viable immediate crisis bridge supply instrument for the Bathinda/Paradip 8-12 day shutdown window — The SPR release operational timeline of 7-14 days (confirmed from ISPRL data cited in the working document itself) means that CCEA authorization granted on April 4 yields crude at refinery no earlier than April 11-18—after the proposed forced shutdown window of April 10-13. The instrument is temporally incompatible with the stated crisis window. SPR is a medium-term (Day 15-30) bridge, not a 72-hour tool. The hypothesis treats it as immediate relief when structural timelines make it irrelevant for the acute phase.
KILLED: IOCL can contract 6-8 West African cargoes loading April 5-15 at $158-163/bbl delivered within 48 hours through Vitol, Trafigura, and Gunvor — Three simultaneous fatal constraints: (1) The MoPNG procurement ceiling of ₹158/bbl requires a price waiver with minimum 48-72 hour approval timeline—competing non-PSU buyers face no such constraint and will clear the spot market before the waiver is granted. (2) The 3-6 prompt cargo estimate per trading house is a structural Q4 2024 estimate that does not reflect crisis-period repositioning—trading houses will have moved their books before IOCL calls. (3) The delivered cost estimate of $158-163/bbl is stale within hours of crisis onset; the analyst's own contrarian identifies $165-172/bbl as the realistic crisis-period range, which means procurement at full run-rate is financially equivalent to or worse than managed run-rate reduction. All three constraints must simultaneously resolve for the hypothesis to work; none is likely to resolve within the stated window.

One-Pager

IOCL and HMEL must secure PMO-level procurement authority, a ₹25,000–35,000 crore fiscal transfer commitment, and ADNOC Fujairah cargo confirmation by April 5—or face LC default by April 10 and Bathinda shutdown by April 11–15.
Situation

At $156/bbl Indian basket versus a ~$124/bbl break-even on frozen retail prices, IOCL and HMEL are burning ₹750–965 crore per day across four refineries with no self-financing path. Bathinda carries 80–85% Russian crude dependency through a single landlocked pipeline and is structurally the first refinery to breach operational minimum—estimated 8–12 days absent confirmed replacement supply—while LPG and ATF safety stocks in the Karnataka/Goa southern grid may exhaust within 5–7 days due to MRPL's shutdown and active panic-buying running 20–30% above normal offtake. If no action is taken by April 5, LC default triggers cross-default across an estimated ₹2,16,700 crore of consolidated PSU debt, Bathinda enters forced shutdown, and southern grid LPG rationing becomes unavoidable within one week.

Analysis
The financing mechanism will fail before physical crude runs out: at $156/bbl, IOCL's 30-day LC obligation (~₹54,000–58,000 crore) exceeds estimated SBI/BoB sanctioned headroom by 40–60%, and post-April 3 Russian crude payments are blocked from Western correspondent banks simultaneously—creating a dual-channel freeze. LC default structurally probable by April 10–13, triggering cross-default across ₹2,16,700 crore consolidated debt; every 24-hour delay in RBI dispensation or MoF sovereign backstop costs ₹750–965 crore in incremental burn with no offsetting revenue path. RBI emergency sectoral exposure dispensation and MoF standing LC authorization for IOCL must be treated as the governing constraint—not sourcing—because physical crude cannot be purchased if the payment instrument is frozen; this track must be activated in parallel with, not after, procurement decisions.
Bathinda's 80–85% single-supplier Russian crude dependency through one landlocked pipeline has zero viable re-routing within 8–12 days; the only physically deliverable non-Hormuz alternative—ADNOC Fujairah pipeline (1.5 mb/d confirmed capacity, 5–7 day Fujairah-to-West India transit)—must have cargo confirmed loaded and in-transit by April 5 or Bathinda's operational minimum is breached before any other sourcing track delivers. Forced Bathinda shutdown below 65% nameplate triggers coker bed damage extending any restart from 10–15 days to 30–45 days—an asymmetric downside worth an estimated ₹3,000–5,000 crore in lost GRM and restart cost at crisis-period crack spreads of $25–35/bbl gasoil. ADNOC Fujairah cargo confirmation (minimum 600,000 barrels loaded by April 5) is the single most time-critical procurement decision in the tree; it is also the only sourcing action whose timeline is compatible with Bathinda's operational window—all other alternatives (West African 25–32 day transit, Brazilian 35–40 day transit) arrive after the shutdown threshold is crossed.
Current PSU procurement authority limits—requiring MoF concurrence and CVC clearance over 5–10 days per contract—are structurally incompatible with the commercial decision speed required; competing non-PSU buyers (Chinese state refiners, Korean and Japanese independents) face no equivalent procedural constraint and will clear available spot cargoes before IOCL price waivers are granted. Every 24-hour procurement delay at ₹750–965 crore/day burn costs ₹3,750–4,825 crore over a 5-day authorization cycle—equivalent to 11–19% of the entire ₹25,000–35,000 crore fiscal transfer requirement, making governance latency a direct financial variable; the West African cargo window (April 5–15 loading) will be closed to PSU buyers operating under standard approval timelines. PMO-level written authority delegating IOCL Chairman procurement approval above ₹5,000 crore without case-by-case MoF concurrence—with explicit Prevention of Corruption Act coverage to remove behavioral paralysis—is a prerequisite for every other sourcing decision to execute; without it, the War Room is a coordination structure with no decision velocity.

CEO Review

REVISE (Clarity: 7/10 | Actionability: 6/10 | Credibility: 5/10)

The analytical horsepower here is real — the financing-before-physical-supply insight is correct and the Bathinda asymmetric downside framing is exactly the right way to prioritize. But I cannot sign this in its current form for three reasons: one, the single most important operational number in the brief — Bathinda's actual crude inventory — is an extrapolation and the team knows it; two, the decisions are structured for a policy document, not for a room where three different ministries need to leave with unambiguous, separated marching orders; and three, the ₹2,16,700 crore cross-default figure will be the first thing Finance Ministry challenges, and if we can't defend it cold, we lose the room before we get to the ask. Get me confirmed Bathinda inventory, source the cross-default number, split the decisions by owner, and pull the retail price hike into its own line. That's a 90-minute revision. Bring it back.

Problems
  • Decision #1 is one sentence containing three separate asks from three separate authorities (PMO, MoF, RBI) — this is not a decision, it's a legislative agenda. Each requires its own owner, approval path, and deadline. Bundling them guarantees diffusion of accountability.
  • The ₹2,16,700 crore cross-default figure is the most dangerous number in this brief and it has zero sourcing footnote. If a journalist or opposition MP asks where that came from and we can't answer in 10 seconds, this entire brief becomes a liability.
  • Bathinda 8–12 days crude cover is explicitly flagged as 'extrapolated' with a live data gap. I am being asked to approve a ₹25,000–35,000 crore commitment and PMO-level authority delegation partly on a number the team admits it does not have. That is not acceptable.
  • '₹160–180 crore/month per dollar above pivot' is internally inconsistent with the daily burn figure. At $32/bbl premium over break-even and ₹160–180 crore per dollar per month, monthly burn should be ~₹5,120–5,760 crore/month — but ₹750–965 crore/day implies ₹22,500–29,000 crore/month. The variance section tries to explain this but it reads as two different metrics mashed together. It will undermine credibility in the room.
  • The ADNOC Fujairah 600,000-barrel figure is presented as the minimum required but there is no calculation showing how 600,000 barrels bridges Bathinda's gap. What is Bathinda's daily crude intake? What does 600k bbls buy in days-cover? This is the single most time-critical action in the brief and the math is missing.
  • Decision #3 contains a retail price recommendation (₹15–20/litre HSD hike) buried in the same paragraph as a War Room constitution request. A retail fuel price increase is a politically explosive, standalone decision that will consume all the oxygen in any room it enters. It cannot be an afterthought in Decision #3.
  • MRPL shutdown is cited as a cause of southern grid LPG stress but there is no explanation of why MRPL is down, whether it is recoverable within the crisis window, or whether restoring it changes the calculus. If MRPL comes back online in 4 days, the southern grid ask changes materially.
  • The brief assumes Russian crude payment channels are fully blocked post-April 3 but Decision #3 simultaneously asks for authorization of CNY/dirham-denominated LC switches for Russian crude. If alternative payment rails exist, why is the Russian crude disruption framed as binary? This is either a hedge that undermines the urgency framing or an incomplete analysis of what the sanctions waiver expiry actually blocks.
  • The War Room composition in Decision #3 excludes MEA, which would be the executing ministry for any ADNOC diplomatic engagement and any sanctions waiver extension negotiation. This is a structural gap.
Missing
  • Confirmed, live Bathinda crude inventory figure from HMEL operations — not an extrapolation. This is non-negotiable before sign-off.
  • A one-paragraph reconciliation of the ₹2,16,700 crore cross-default figure: which debt instruments, which cross-default clauses, verified by which legal/financial authority.
  • Bathinda daily crude intake (barrels/day) so the 600,000-barrel ADNOC cargo can be validated as sufficient for the claimed days-cover bridge.
  • Status of MRPL shutdown: cause, estimated restoration timeline, impact on southern grid LPG scenario if restored within the crisis window.
  • Has any preliminary contact been made with ADNOC? Is the Fujairah cargo physically available or is this a theoretical sourcing option? 'Must confirm by April 5' is meaningless if ADNOC hasn't been approached.
  • What happens if PMO authority delegation is granted but the ADNOC cargo is not available — is there a fallback sourcing option with any timeline compatibility with Bathinda's window, even a partial one?
  • Legal opinion on PC Act coverage for emergency procurement — even a one-line confirmation that the Law Ministry has been consulted, because without it, IOCL Chairman will not move regardless of what PMO writes.
  • Scenario sensitivity: what does the burn rate and default timeline look like if crude drops back to $130/bbl? The entire brief is premised on $156/bbl holding — is there a range?
