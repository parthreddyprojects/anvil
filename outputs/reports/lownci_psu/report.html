Low-Complexity PSU Refiners: Crisis Brief

Decision Deadline: April 3, 2026 | Indian Basket: $156/bbl
Problem Statement

India's six low-complexity PSU refineries face an 8–25 day cascade shutdown window: Hormuz mining has blocked 35–40% of seaborne crude, the Indian basket is at $156/bbl against a $142/bbl viability threshold, MRPL has already shut as a confirmed behavioral precedent, and the April 3 Russia waiver lapse threatens the remaining low-cost supply leg. Without a binding fiscal backstop and simultaneous physical supply activation within 72 hours, each refinery CMD faces the individually rational but collectively catastrophic decision to initiate controlled shutdown. The crisis is not a crude sourcing problem alone — it is a sequenced failure of financial authorization, supply logistics, and inter-ministerial coordination that must be solved as a single indivisible package or not at all.

Decision Sensitivity: The pivot point is $142/bbl on the Indian basket (approximately a $14/bbl drop from current $156): below that level, operating at 40–50% capacity with available non-Hormuz crudes generates positive gross refining margin even without a government subsidy top-up, making continued operation the dominan

Core Hypotheses

H1
A Presidential Directive indemnifying PSU refinery boards from personal liability for operating at negative GRM, paired with a sovereign LC guarantee line of ₹40,000–50,000 crore ($4.8–5.9B), must be issued within 48 hours (by April 2, 23:59 IST) to prevent the individually rational but collectively catastrophic decision by each refinery CMD to initiate controlled shutdown — without this fiscal backstop, all six refineries will begin controlled shutdown procedures before April 7 regardless of crude availability.
Break point: Indian basket crude drops to or below $142/bbl, at which point 40–50% nameplate throughput on available non-Hormuz crudes generates positive GRM without government intervention, eliminating the need for a fiscal backstop and making continued operation the dominant strategy without the Directive | Status: CONFIRMED (MEDIUM)
H2
ONGC must be directed within 24 hours to maximize Bombay High Light allocation to HPCL Mumbai and BPCL Mumbai, delivering 15–30 kbd of incremental compatible crude via existing shore terminal pipeline within 48–72 hours — this is the only intervention physically capable of preventing the two highest-risk refineries from breaching their 7-day operational shutdown trigger before any seaborne alternative cargo can arrive.
Break point: If HPCL Mumbai and BPCL Mumbai have confirmed crude inventory cover exceeding 15 days (above GoI guideline minimum), the urgency of ONGC reallocation drops from emergency-critical to precautionary, and the 24-hour clock becomes a 5–7 day planning horizon instead | Status: UNCERTAIN (MEDIUM)
H3
MEA must open a G2G emergency channel to ADNOC within 24 hours to activate Fujairah (ADCOP bypass) crude supply for BPCL Kochi and HPCL/BPCL Mumbai — a G2G-confirmed Fujairah cargo of 1–2 MMbbl loading within 72 hours can reach Kochi in 6–8 days and Mumbai in 7–9 days, making it the only seaborne non-Hormuz supply option deliverable before runway expiry at Kochi and barely within runway at Mumbai.
Break point: If UAE confirms that ADCOP pipeline throughput is already at maximum utilization serving UAE domestic demand and existing contracts (i.e., no discretionary Fujairah export volume is available within the 72-hour loading window), this hypothesis is killed and West African cargo becomes the primary seaborne alternative despite its 18–22 day voyage time disadvantage | Status: UNCERTAIN (LOW)
H4
India must NOT allow a clean lapse of Russian crude access on April 3 without simultaneously confirming alternative volume sufficiency — the crisis cell should instruct MEA to engage Washington within 24 hours to negotiate a 60-day rolling forbearance extension, because the post-April 3 Russian crude exposure for these six refineries (estimated 30–45% of crude diet each) cannot be replaced at equivalent volume and price within the 72-hour window, and a clean lapse adds $14–20/bbl to effective crude cost on Russian volumes by forcing shadow fleet and alternative payment routing costs that push the Indian basket from $156/bbl further away from the $142/bbl viability threshold.
Break point: If Washington confirms within 48 hours that secondary sanctions enforcement will not target Indian PSU entities for Russian crude transactions entered into before May 31 (a 60-day grace period), the April 3 cliff is defused and Russian crude continuity is preserved as the low-cost supply anchor — conversely, if US confirms immediate enforcement of secondary sanctions post-April 3 with no grace period, the hypothesis flips and Russian crude must be suspended immediately to protect PSU bank access to dollar clearing | Status: UNCERTAIN (LOW)
H5
BPCL Kochi must be designated the emergency regional hub refinery operating at ≥70% nameplate (≥215 kbd) with priority crude allocation — not reduced to minimum run-rate — because its role as the sole viable product backstop for MRPL's Karnataka/Goa/Kerala territory (covering ATF supply to two airports, 70–75% of Goa's petroleum needs, and 40–45% of Karnataka HSD) makes its throughput a public safety obligation that supersedes individual refinery financial optimization, and its VLCC-capable SBM and moderate complexity (NCI ~9.2) make it the system's most efficient emergency crude receiver and processor.
Break point: If BPCL Kochi's own crude inventory drops below 8 days cover without a confirmed Fujairah or West African cargo in transit, the hub designation must be reversed and Kochi must be reduced to 40–50% nameplate to preserve its runway — at that point, emergency product imports (diesel, LPG) via coastal shipping from BPCL Mumbai or CPCL Manali must substitute for Kochi's MRPL coverage role | Status: UNCERTAIN (LOW)
H6
NRL Numaligarh must be ring-fenced from the central crisis response and managed separately under OIL India/ONGC Assam domestic crude priority allocation — its structural insulation (primarily processes domestic Assam waxy crude, 14–16 day runway versus 10–12 days for Mumbai refineries, no Hormuz or Russian crude dependency at scale) and its critical role as the sole refinery serving upper Assam and Arunachal Pradesh mean that diverting NRL crude or operational resources to solve the west coast crisis would create a northeast India fuel emergency in exchange for marginal west coast benefit.
Break point: If Assam domestic crude production (OIL India + ONGC combined ~70–77 kbd) falls below NRL's minimum run-rate requirement of 25–30 kbd due to production disruption or pipeline failure on the Brahmaputra corridor, NRL can no longer be ring-fenced and requires emergency ESPO allocation via Paradip pipeline — at that point NRL joins the central crisis response queue and its northeast India supply obligations must be managed via emergency road/rail product tanker dispatch | Status: CONFIRMED (HIGH)
H7
MoPNG must issue an emergency demand rationing directive within 6 hours — before any other intervention — mandating a 15–20% reduction in retail fuel dispensing across Karnataka, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, and Kerala through pump-level allocation caps, because demand rationing is the only sub-24-hour tool available to extend product inventory runway at all six refineries simultaneously and buys 3–5 additional days of runway that determine whether the Fujairah G2G cargo (6–8 days) arrives before or after the first shutdown trigger is breached.
Break point: If product depot inventories at state-level distribution points confirm more than 12 days of cover at current dispensing rates (i.e., downstream buffer is larger than refinery crude buffer), then demand rationing is not the critical path item and the 6-hour urgency drops — the crisis is upstream (crude supply) rather than downstream (product supply), and rationing creates political cost without operational benefit | Status: UNCERTAIN (LOW)
H8
IOCL Bina must be designated the priority inland buffer refinery and maintained at ≥60% nameplate throughput (≥95 kbd) through emergency ESPO allocation via Paradip pipeline, because its pipeline-fed supply to the landlocked Hindi heartland states (MP, UP, Rajasthan) cannot be substituted by coastal product movement within any crisis timeframe, and its 14-day crude runway gives a viable window to secure ESPO cargo via Paradip before the shutdown trigger — but only if Kandla/Mundra port throughput is pre-cleared and pipeline allocation is ring-fenced from NRL diversion within 48 hours.
Break point: If ESPO post-April 3 faces confirmed secondary sanctions enforcement on Indian PSU bank payment channels (H4 killed), Bina's primary alternative crude source is eliminated and it must be reduced to minimum 40% nameplate on whatever non-Hormuz spot cargo can be secured via Paradip — below this threshold, MP/UP/Rajasthan inland HSD and LPG distribution collapses within 20–25 days as no coastal refinery can supply these markets by road/rail within operational timeframes | Status: UNCERTAIN (LOW)

Hypothesis Graveyard

KILLED: The MEA-ADNOC Fujairah G2G channel can deliver a loading-confirmed 1-2 MMbbl cargo within 72 hours at prices that don't worsen the GRM crisis, arriving within BPCL Kochi's runway — The combination of: (1) ADNOC's commercial incentive to maximize spot pricing in a seller's market crisis rather than provide G2G emergency allocations at concessional prices, (2) the joint probability of ADCOP discretionary availability + 72-hour loading slot + 6-8 day voyage (not 10-12) all being favorable simultaneously is LOW, and (3) if cargo arrives at $175-185/bbl effective cost due to crisis premium, it deepens under-recovery beyond the fiscal backstop's ₹40,000-50,000 crore ceiling, potentially making the cargo worse than no cargo from a solvency standpoint. The hypothesis survives as a diplomatic action item but fails as an operational solution.
KILLED: A 15-20% retail fuel dispensing cap across four states can be operationally implemented within 6 hours and will produce a net 3-5 day runway extension — The announcement effect (panic buying in the first 48-72 hours exceeding compliance-driven reduction), combined with the near-certainty of non-compliance by at least two of four target state governments, means the net demand effect in the first 48 hours is directionally negative, not positive. The 3-5 day runway extension is an EXTRAPOLATED MEDIUM confidence estimate that assumes compliance — without compliance, the estimate is invalid. The hypothesis survives as a theoretical tool but fails in the specific political environment described.

One-Pager

MoPNG must issue a Presidential Directive plus RBI bank circular as a co-equal two-key instrument within 48 hours, simultaneously activating ONGC Mumbai High reallocation and MEA-ADNOC G2G engagement, or all six refineries enter controlled shutdown within 8–25 days regardless of crude availability.
Situation

At $156/bbl Indian basket — $14/bbl above the $142/bbl GRM breakeven — every day of operation deepens PSU balance sheet losses by an estimated ₹400–600 crore system-wide with no board-level legal protection, and MRPL's LC refusal sequence confirms this is the template each remaining CMD will follow. HPCL Mumbai and BPCL Mumbai carry only 10–12 days of crude inventory cover, breaching the 7-day operational shutdown trigger within 3–5 days absent new cargo arrivals; BPCL Kochi has 13 days, CPCL Manali 12–13 days, IOCL Bina 14 days, and NRL 14–16 days. If nothing is done by April 3, the cascade proceeds: HPCL and BPCL Mumbai shut within days 3–5, eliminating 390 kbd of West Coast capacity and triggering LPG and HSD supply-outs across Maharashtra; BPCL Kochi follows within days 7–13, collapsing ATF supply to Goa and Mangalore airports and the Karnataka HSD grid already exposed by MRPL's shutdown; IOCL Bina and NRL follow within days 14–25, creating unsubstitutable inland supply failure across MP, UP, Rajasthan, and the strategically sensitive northeast border territories.

Analysis
The fiscal authorization failure is a two-key problem, not one: a Presidential Directive indemnifying PSU refinery CMDs from personal liability for operating at negative GRM is necessary but insufficient without a simultaneous RBI emergency circular under Section 35A instructing PSU banks to issue sovereign-guaranteed LCs despite exposure concentration norm breaches — without the RBI instrument, crude suppliers receive no payment guarantee and will not load cargoes regardless of CMD indemnification PSU banks (SBI, PNB, Bank of Baroda) are legally entitled to refuse LC issuance on RBI prudential grounds even after a Presidential Directive is issued to refinery CMDs; at $156/bbl, each VLCC cargo LC (~$80–120M notional) issued against an operationally insolvent PSU buyer constitutes an unsecured contingent liability that RBI's statutory exposure norms prohibit without a formal Section 35A override — a 24-hour delay in the RBI instrument relative to the Presidential Directive is sufficient to block the first emergency cargo LC and trigger HPCL Mumbai's shutdown sequence The crisis cell must treat the Presidential Directive and RBI Section 35A circular as a single simultaneous instrument requiring PMO-level coordination with Finance Ministry and RBI Governor within the same 48-hour window — any sequential issuance where the RBI circular lags the Directive by even 12–24 hours structurally fails the intervention
The ONGC Mumbai High reallocation is the only physically deliverable supply intervention within HPCL and BPCL Mumbai's 3–5 day remaining buffer window, but the claimed 15–30 kbd of incremental volume above existing commitments is in direct tension with PSC allocation obligations to Reliance Industries and Nayara Energy that a ministerial direction cannot unilaterally override without triggering immediate commercial arbitration Mumbai High produces 180–185 kbd total against combined Mumbai refinery minimum run-rate requirement of ~165–195 kbd — if Reliance and Nayara PSC allocations are legally protected, the available incremental volume above existing commitments may be 5–15 kbd rather than 15–30 kbd, extending HPCL Mumbai's runway by only 1–2 days rather than the 3–4 days required to bridge to any seaborne cargo arrival; the gap between a successful and failed ONGC reallocation is the difference between Mumbai refineries surviving to receive a Fujairah or West African cargo versus entering shutdown before it arrives MoPNG Petroleum Secretary must within 6 hours obtain a legal opinion on PSC allocation ring-fencing, simultaneously directing ONGC to identify genuinely uncommitted Mumbai High volumes and, if incremental volume is confirmed below 10 kbd, immediately activating supply-side rationing through PSU oil company depot allocation orders (not retail pump caps) to extend effective product runway by 3–4 days as the substitute bridge mechanism
IOCL Bina's central India supply role is structurally insolvent if ESPO access lapses post-April 3, because no non-Hormuz, non-Russian crude alternative can arrive via Paradip-Kandla pipeline within Bina's 14-day runway — West African voyage to Paradip is 18–22 days plus 3–4 days pipeline transit, and Kandla port prioritization of crude tankers over fertilizer vessels during the Rabi-Kharif agricultural transition requires Cabinet-level inter-ministerial resolution that cannot be achieved within 48 hours A Bina shutdown eliminates the sole refinery supplying landlocked MP, UP, and Rajasthan — approximately 150–200 million people with no coastal product import alternative — and triggers HSD and LPG distribution failure within 20–25 days, cascading into agricultural operations failure (April–May irrigation pump fuel) and food supply chain disruption across five states within 30–40 days; the combined H4 failure (ESPO lapse) plus H8 failure (no viable alternative supply within runway) is the only crisis scenario that escalates from an energy emergency to a humanitarian emergency MEA must engage Washington within 24 hours specifically to secure a 60-day ESPO grace period covering cargoes loaded before May 31 — not as a diplomatic courtesy but as the sole supply pathway that keeps Bina operational within its runway; simultaneously, MoPNG must place a spot ESPO loading order at Kozmino within 72 hours contingent on forbearance confirmation, accepting that if Washington refuses, Bina must be reduced to 40% nameplate on available non-Hormuz spot cargo and emergency road-rail product dispatch from CPCL Manali and BPCL Kochi must be pre-positioned as the central India fallback

CEO Review

REVISE (Clarity: 8/10 | Actionability: 7/10 | Credibility: 6/10)

This brief is analytically serious and the underlying crisis logic is coherent — the two-key fiscal authorization framing is genuinely insightful and whoever wrote the IOCL Bina humanitarian escalation scenario understands second-order consequences. But I cannot sign off on a ₹40,000–50,000 crore sovereign exposure authorization when the central loss figure doesn't pass a back-of-envelope check, the most critical physical supply number is self-described as potentially wrong by two-thirds, and not a single accountable individual is named anywhere in three decisions. The brief is written as if precision of language substitutes for verification of facts — 'estimated,' 'approximately,' 'confirmed behavioral precedent' without the confirmation shown. Revise with: (1) a named ONGC operations contact who has actually been called, (2) a derived daily loss figure that checks out arithmetically, (3) a named accountable officer per decision, and (4) a single hard deadline date at the top. Bring it back in 4 hours. The urgency framing may be correct — but urgency is not a substitute for a number I can defend to the Finance Minister.

Problems
  • The $142/bbl GRM breakeven figure is load-bearing for the entire crisis narrative and is never sourced or derived. If that number is wrong by even $5, the urgency calculus collapses. I need a footnote or a CFO sign-off on it.
  • MRPL shutdown is cited as 'confirmed behavioral precedent' but zero details are given — when did it shut, what was the trigger sequence, what was the actual LC refusal chain? If this is the template, I need to see the template, not just be told it exists.
  • The $156/bbl Indian basket price is presented as current fact. What is the source and as-of date? Crude prices move intraday. A brief asking me to authorize ₹40,000–50,000 crore in sovereign LC guarantees cannot have a stale or unattributed crude price as its foundation.
  • The ₹400–600 crore/day system-wide loss estimate has no derivation shown. At $156/bbl vs $142/bbl breakeven, that's $14/bbl loss across what throughput assumption? Back-calculate: 390 kbd West Coast alone at $14/bbl is roughly $5.5M/day or ₹46 crore/day — nowhere near ₹400–600 crore. Either the throughput assumption is much larger or the loss rate per barrel is higher than $14. This number does not pass a back-of-envelope check and undermines credibility of every other number in the brief.
  • ONGC Mumbai High reallocation volume (15–30 kbd claimed, possibly only 5–15 kbd available) is the single most critical physical intervention for the 3–5 day window, and the brief itself admits the number may be wrong by 50–67%. This is not a footnote problem — it means Decision #2 may be operationally useless before it is signed. The brief should not present this as a viable intervention without a preliminary ONGC operations call already completed.
  • The RBI Section 35A mechanism is described as if it is a routine tool. Has RBI ever used Section 35A for this purpose? Is there legal precedent? Citing a specific statutory provision without confirming its applicability to sovereign LC guarantee backstops is a credibility risk — if the RBI Governor's counsel says Section 35A doesn't cover this use case, the entire fiscal backstop architecture fails and this brief has no Plan B.
  • Decision #3 asks MEA to simultaneously negotiate with ADNOC and Washington within 24–48 hours on two completely different diplomatic tracks with completely different geopolitical sensitivities. There is no prioritization between these tracks, no fallback if both fail simultaneously, and no acknowledgment that a U.S. ESPO forbearance request framed as 'humanitarian' rather than 'sanctions negotiation' is a characterization Washington will immediately see through. The diplomatic ask is naive in its framing.
  • The 8–25 day cascade window is presented as precise but the range is so wide (17-day spread) that it is operationally meaningless for sequencing. The brief needs a single worst-case trigger date — the day HPCL Mumbai hits 7-day cover — stated explicitly as THE deadline, not buried in a range.
  • No owner is named for any decision. 'PMO must,' 'MoPNG must,' 'MEA must' — who specifically? Which individual is accountable? I cannot approve asks with no named accountable officer.
Missing
  • CFO or MoPNG joint secretary sign-off on the $142/bbl breakeven figure — this is the crisis trigger and it needs a name attached to it.
  • MRPL shutdown chronology: exact date, LC refusal sequence, legal basis CMD used, regulatory response to date. If this is the behavioral template, show me the actual sequence.
  • A confirmed ONGC operations assessment — even a 2-hour phone call — on genuinely uncommitted Mumbai High volumes before Decision #2 is brought to me. I will not approve a physical supply intervention built on a number the brief itself says may be 67% smaller than stated.
  • Legal confirmation that RBI Section 35A is the correct statutory instrument for sovereign LC backstop authorization, with at least a preliminary opinion from RBI's legal department or MoF's DFSIP.
  • Named accountable officers for each of the three decisions — not ministries, specific individuals with mobile numbers.
  • A single hard deadline date and time stated explicitly as THE shutdown trigger for the highest-risk asset (HPCL Mumbai), not a range.
  • What has already been attempted? Has MoPNG already called ADNOC? Has Finance Ministry already spoken to RBI? If nothing has been done, why is this the first briefing? If things have been attempted and failed, I need to know that immediately.
  • A contingency if all three decisions fail or are delayed past their windows — the brief says this must be solved 'as a single indivisible package or not at all' but then provides partial fallbacks inside individual decisions, which is contradictory. Give me a clean worst-case consequence statement with a timeframe.
