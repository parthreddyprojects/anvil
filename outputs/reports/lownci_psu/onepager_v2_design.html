Situation
One refinery has already shut (MRPL); six more — HPCL Mumbai, BPCL Mumbai, BPCL Kochi, CPCL Manali (Chennai), IOCL Bina, and NRL Numaligarh — follow within 4 days because refiners are losing ₹500 crore daily and have no legal cover to keep operating. Maharashtra and Karnataka lose fuel first — then landlocked central India, triggering a humanitarian crisis the ₹45,000 crore backstop prevents.

Analysis
01
Two legal instruments must be issued at the same moment: a Presidential Directive protecting refinery heads at HPCL Mumbai, BPCL Mumbai, BPCL Kochi, CPCL Manali, IOCL Bina, and NRL Numaligarh from personal liability for operating at negative margins — and an emergency Reserve Bank directive allowing banks to issue government-backed payment guarantees to crude suppliers.
A 24-hour gap between the two blocks the first emergency payment and triggers HPCL Mumbai's shutdown on schedule.
PMO must treat both as one indivisible package — issuing them separately is not sequencing, it is failure.
02
The only crude physically reachable by HPCL Mumbai and BPCL Mumbai within 4 days is domestic Bombay High Light from ONGC — delivered via existing offshore platform → shore terminal → refinery pipeline with zero new infrastructure. But existing term contracts may legally cap how much volume can be redirected from current buyers.
If redirectable volume is below 10,000 barrels per day, both Mumbai refineries gain only 1–2 extra days — insufficient to bridge to any incoming seaborne cargo.
The Petroleum Secretary must confirm available ONGC volume by 18:00 IST April 2 (in 6 days); if insufficient, activate 15% fuel rationing across Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, and Kerala the same night.
03
IOCL Bina — the sole fuel supplier for landlocked Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, and Rajasthan, with no coastal or pipeline alternative — loses access to its Russian crude shipment on April 3 (in 7 days) when the US sanctions waiver expires, with no replacement source currently contracted.
A Bina shutdown cuts fuel to approximately 200 million people; within 30 days, agricultural and food supply chains fail across five states — this is the scenario that converts an energy crisis into a humanitarian emergency.
This requires a direct Foreign Secretary phone call within 24 hours (by March 28), not a written diplomatic note — the response timeline is incompatible with paper process.
