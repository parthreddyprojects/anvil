Analysis
01
Two legal instruments must be issued at the same moment: a Presidential Directive protecting refinery heads from personal liability for operating at a loss, and an emergency Reserve Bank directive allowing banks to issue government-backed payment guarantees to crude suppliers.
A 24-hour gap between the two blocks the first emergency payment and triggers HPCL (Hindustan Petroleum) Mumbai's shutdown.
PMO must treat both as one package — the decision sequence is the risk.
02
The only crude that can physically reach Mumbai's refineries within 4 days is domestic output from state-owned ONGC — but existing contracts may legally cap how much can be redirected.
If redirectable volume is below 10,000 barrels per day (about 8% of Mumbai's daily refinery need), Mumbai refineries gain only 2 extra days — not enough to bridge to any incoming cargo.
Petroleum Secretary confirms available ONGC volume by 18:00 IST April 2; if below threshold, activates four-state fuel rationing by 20:00 IST same night.
03
IOCL (Indian Oil Corporation) Bina — the sole fuel supplier for landlocked Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, and Rajasthan — loses access to its Russian crude shipment on April 3 with no alternative source in place. The US sanctions waiver expiry on April 3, 23:59 IST removes the last legal cover for this supply route.
A Bina shutdown cuts fuel to approximately 200 million people; within 30 days, agricultural and food supply chains fail across five states.
This is the one scenario that turns an energy crisis into a humanitarian emergency — it requires a direct diplomatic call within 24 hours, not a written note.
