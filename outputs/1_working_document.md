# STRATEGIC BRIEFING: IRAN-HORMUZ CRISIS — DAY 25\n**Date: March 24, 2026 | Classification: C-Suite Eyes Only**\n\n---\n\n## SECTION A — WHAT HAPPENED (Day 20-25)\n\n### A1. What is the Strait of Hormuz and why does 42% of India's crude flow through it?\n\nThe Strait of Hormuz is a narrow waterway between Iran and Oman connecting the Persian Gulf to the Gulf of Oman and the open ocean. Its two unidirectional sea lanes facilitate transit of approximately **20 million barrels of oil per day** -- roughly **20% of all global seaborne oil trade** -- plus 20% of the world's LNG transported by sea. For India specifically, over **50% of crude imports** (from Iraq, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, UAE) and **90% of LPG imports** transit through this chokepoint. India imported an average of **2.6 million barrels per day** from Gulf countries prior to the crisis. The strait's importance is structural: there are no pipeline alternatives at comparable scale -- existing bypass pipelines (Saudi East-West, UAE ADCOP) carry only **3.5-5.5 million bbl/d combined** versus 20 million through the strait.\n\n### A2. What triggered the closure?\n\nOn **February 28, 2026**, the United States and Israel conducted extensive joint strikes across Iran, killing **Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei** and national security chief **Ali Larijani**. Iran retaliated by declaring closure of the Strait of Hormuz. On **March 2**, a senior IRGC official confirmed the strait was officially closed, threatening any ship attempting passage. On **March 5**, Iran refined its position: the strait would remain closed only to ships from the US, Israel, and Western allies, while allowing selective passage for non-aligned nations. The crisis was preceded by failed nuclear negotiations in Geneva and a prior 12-day air conflict in 2025.\n\n### A3. How much is offline -- and has it gotten worse since Day 20?\n\nFlows through the Strait of Hormuz have **plunged from ~20 million bbl/d to a trickle**. This is the largest single supply disruption in the history of the global oil market. Since Day 20 (March 19), the situation has **worsened with the South Pars strike** (see A5) and Iran's retaliatory attacks on Gulf energy infrastructure. The **March 18 Israeli strike on South Pars** knocked out ~12% of Iran's gas production. Iran retaliated by striking Qatar's **Ras Laffan Industrial City**, causing \"extensive damage\" per QatarEnergy. Iran also halted gas flows to Iraq, which depends on Iran for 33-40% of its gas and power. However, **limited passage has resumed** for select vessels -- two Indian LPG tankers transited on March 23.\n\n### A4. How does this compare to every prior oil shock?\n\nThe IEA head **Fatih Birol** stated this crisis has had **\"a worse impact on oil than the two oil shocks of the 1970s combined\"** and a worse effect on gas markets than the Russia-Ukraine war. Specific comparisons:\n\n| Crisis | Supply Knocked Offline | Price Impact |\n|---|---|---|\n| **1973 Arab Embargo** | 5-7% of global supply over 5 months | $3 to $11.65 (+289%) |\n| **1979 Iranian Revolution** | 4.8M bbl/d (7% of world production) | Prices doubled |\n| **1990 Gulf War** | Brief spike, 9 months | Moderate, temporary |\n| **2026 Hormuz Crisis** | **~20M bbl/d (20% of global supply)** | **Brent: ~$75 to $126 peak (+68%)** |\n\nThe scale is unprecedented: the 1973 embargo removed 5-7% of supply; this crisis removes **20%**.\n\n### A5. What does the South Pars strike mean?\n\nOn **March 18, 2026**, Israel struck Iran's **South Pars gas field** -- the **world's largest natural gas field**, responsible for **70% of Iran's gas production**. The strike damaged petrochemical facilities at **Asaluyeh**, including storage tanks, gas installations, and refineries, knocking out **~12% of Iran's total gas production**. This was a deliberate escalation into energy warfare. Iran retaliated **the same day** by striking **Qatar's Ras Laffan** (the field is shared -- Qatar calls its side \"North Dome\"), causing \"extensive damage.\" Brent jumped from $103 to $108 on the news. Iran also cut gas exports to Iraq, triggering power shortages. European gas prices rose 7%. This attack transformed the conflict from a military confrontation into a **full-spectrum energy war**.\n\n### A6. Trump's Hormuz ultimatum then pause -- what's real?\n\n**Timeline:**\n- **March 21 (Saturday):** Trump issued a **48-hour ultimatum** to Iran -- reopen the Strait of Hormuz or the US would \"obliterate\" Iran's power plants.\n- **March 23 (Monday), morning:** With the deadline expiring, Trump posted on Truth Social (all caps): *\"I AM PLEASED TO REPORT THAT THE UNITED STATES OF AMERICA, AND THE COUNTRY OF IRAN, HAVE HAD, OVER THE LAST TWO DAYS, VERY GOOD AND PRODUCTIVE CONVERSATIONS REGARDING A COMPLETE AND TOTAL RESOLUTION OF OUR HOSTILITIES IN THE MIDDLE EAST.\"*\n- He ordered the **\"Department of War to postpone any and all military strikes against Iranian power plants and energy infrastructure for a five day period, subject to the success of the ongoing meetings and discussions.\"**\n- To reporters: *\"We have major points of agreement, I would say, almost all points of agreement.\"*\n- **Conditions stated:** Iran halting uranium enrichment; US removing enriched uranium already in Iran.\n- **Envoys:** Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner held discussions with a \"top person\" on the Iranian side.\n- **The pause expires March 28 (Saturday).**\n\n### A7. Parliament Speaker **Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf** posted: \"no negotiations have been held with the US.\" Foreign Ministry spokesman **Esmaeil Baghaei** denied discussions but critically admitted that **\"messages have been received from some friendly countries regarding the US's request for negotiations\"** -- confirming indirect communications.\n\n**The mediators are:**\n- **Egypt** -- Egyptian intelligence established a **direct channel with the IRGC** (critical after Larijani's killing removed Iran's usual back-channel negotiator)\n- **Oman** -- Pursuing a separate track on **safe passage arrangements** for the strait; Oman has historically been the primary back-channel between Washington and Tehran\n- **Turkey** -- Confirmed by CNN as actively mediating\n- **Saudi Arabia** -- Gulf states facing direct exposure to any escalation\n- **Pakistan** -- Trump held a call with Pakistan's army chief **Gen. What does 88% import dependence mean for 1.4 billion people?\n\nIndia's oil import dependency reached **88.5-88.6%** in the first 10 months of FY26. India is the **world's third-largest crude consumer**. Every **$10/bbl rise** adds **$14-16 billion annually** to India's import bill. With prices up ~$80 from pre-crisis levels (~$75), the additional burden is a staggering **~$120 billion annualized**. This translates directly to: higher transport costs for 1.4 billion people, food inflation (diesel powers logistics/agriculture), fertilizer shortages (gas-dependent), LPG cooking fuel scarcity for 300+ million households, and current account deficit pressure that weakens the rupee further in a vicious cycle.\n\n### B7. LPG crisis -- worse or better? Rationing update?\n\n**Worse, but managed.** Key developments:\n- **March 10:** Government invoked **ESMA (Essential Services Maintenance Act)** -- ordered refineries to maximize LPG output using propane/butane streams.\n- **March 12:** Centre imposed a **20% cap on commercial LPG supply** to prioritize domestic household use and prevent hoarding/black marketing.\n- India imports **60% of its LPG**, and **90% of LPG imports** passed through Hormuz pre-crisis.\n- The **Outlook India** describes \"Queues, Chaos, and An Empty Cylinder\" in ground-level reporting.\n- Government has ordered **gas allocation to refineries cut to 65%** of past 6-month consumption.\n- Fertilizer plants guaranteed **70% of past 6-month average** gas consumption.\n\n### B9. ISPRL has **3.372 MMT** of crude (64% of 5.33 MMT total capacity) across Visakhapatnam, Mangaluru, and Padur. PM Modi stated SPR capacity has grown to 53 lakh MT, with expansion to 65+ lakh MT underway.\n\n**Total reserves (SPR + commercial stocks at refineries):** ~**74 days** of import cover, but this is **declining** as refineries draw down commercial stocks.\n\n**For context:** Japan has **260 days**, South Korea **210 days**, China **104-115 days**, US **200 days**. India's 9.5 days of dedicated strategic reserves is dangerously thin.\n\n### B10.
